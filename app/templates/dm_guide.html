Your Manage pages

Everything for running {{ here.name if here else "the campaign" }} is under Manage, in the menu bar. They always act on the campaign in the address, so they can't change the wrong one.

  • Needs you: what's waiting, most important first. Start here.
  • Inbox: suggestions from the table and Claude's drafts.
  • Sessions: draft, review and publish each session's recap.
  • Table: who's at the table and what they play. Add people who have an account and their characters, change them, or remove them; Left the table brings them back. Someone brand new needs an admin.
  • House rules: your rulings on how askdnd rolls and shows things.
  • History: every change in this campaign, with Restore.
  • Settings: the campaign's name, your name as Claude writes it in recaps, Books: the rules books you share with the table on D&D Beyond, each on or off for this campaign (so far askdnd holds the 2024 Monster Manual's Beasts, which join Wild Shape's forms labelled MM 2024; off, druids see only the free rules' Beasts), Features: Sync with D&D Beyond, everything that changes in play kept in step with each player's D&D Beyond sheet (tell the table first: D&D Beyond's terms don't allow tools like this), and Labs: features still being tried, such as askdnd's own VTT, each with its own toggle, what it does and where it is on the roadmap (switching one off puts the page back; nothing the table did is lost).

At the table: your D&D Beyond as a backup

Where the campaign has The DM as a backup (part of Sync with D&D Beyond, which you switch on in Settings › Features), a player who forgets to open their sheet doesn't stop the game. While you have any D&D Beyond page open in Chrome with the askdnd bridge (the VTT counts), your browser makes the changes askdnd sends for a character whose own sheet is closed: damage, healing, slots, conditions and the rest. D&D Beyond lets a campaign's DM change its players' sheets, so it uses your sign-in, and only for characters in that D&D Beyond campaign: put its id in Settings (D&D Beyond campaign id), or nothing is made through you. A player's sheet you have open yourself counts as one of your pages, not as theirs. Nothing asks you first: they're the changes the player or you made in askdnd, and a player's own open sheet always goes first.

Your Live pill counts them ("4 of 4 connected · 1 through you", yellow), and the Live window says which sheet is made through you, with a Made through you list. If a sheet can't be reached while the table plays, the pill is red and the window offers to open D&D Beyond. The player's pill stays yellow and asks them to open their sheet, as their rolls reach D&D Beyond's Game Log only from there. D&D Beyond doesn't say who made a change; askdnd's log says "made through the DM".

askdnd's table: places, maps and your scene notes

Where the campaign has askdnd's VTT on (Labs, in Settings; being built, so the table still plays on D&D Beyond's VTT), Table in the menu bar is yours to set up. A place is one map (or none) with its tokens and its own fog, like a Location in D&D Beyond's Maps. Add one with + Place, then Map ▾ › Add a map: a picture (then set its grid: how many squares across) or a Dungeon Alchemist file (.dd2vtt: its grid and walls come with it). Place the party puts everyone's token on it; + Creatures adds creatures from your books and the SRD, several at once, hidden if you like. Players see only the place you Show to players.

Your scene notes are pins on a place's map (+ Scene pin, then click where), each with your sections: the read-aloud first (shown boxed), Purpose, Outcomes, Location info, Activities and Scene summaries, all optional; type @ and a scene's name to link it. They're yours alone: not the players, not reviewers, not admins (Brett included), and never sent to Claude.

Start a fight takes in every token on the map. Your creatures are rolled for you (one roll for a group of the same creature; type a number on any to split it off), the players roll their own, then Begin Round 1 and Next turn. Press a creature for its stat block, its Hit Points (type −7, +5 or a number) and its rolls, which only you see unless you pick otherwise. ⋯ has Undo, Battle settings (what players see of a creature's Hit Points, how diagonals count) and End the fight.

Fog ▾: turn fog on and players see only what you show; drag over squares to show or hide them, click in a room to show it whole (a Dungeon Alchemist map's walls and doors bound it), or show and hide everything. Each place keeps its own fog, and a creature under fog never reaches a player's screen. Terrain ▾: drag over Difficult Terrain; moves count it double. While anyone drags a token the ruler says its feet; in a fight, leaving a creature's reach puts a card up for you and that player (an Opportunity Attack is possible: your ruling). Area draws a spell's area for everyone, with who's in it; Clear areas takes them all away. Click a token and the arrow keys move it; ? lists the keys (N is Next turn).

The idea: you own what the table reads

Recaps, key moments, people, places, factions, items, quests and the glossary are the campaign record. Three things feed it, and nothing from Claude or a player reaches the table until you (or a reviewer) say yes:

Brett's notes and recordings→ Claude drafts →You review→The table
Players' suggestions→You decide→The table
What you write→ straight to →The table

Nothing is ever lost: every change is kept on Manage › History with Restore.

Tips
  • Go in order. Each draft builds on the recaps before it, so earlier fixes make later drafts better.
  • Small bites work. One or two sessions before each game night clears it in a few weeks.
  • Good enough is fine. Everything stays editable, and History keeps every earlier version.

After a game night (once the backlog is done)

  1. Brett records the session; the Processor on his Mac removes table talk and sends only the game to askdnd.
  2. A draft appears on Sessions within a few minutes.
  3. Review and publish it, as above. Before the next session is ideal, so the table's Previously on… is ready.

The inbox: suggestions and Claude's drafts

Everything waiting for your OK is on Manage › Inbox. An email tells you once a day (at 6 PM) when something new has arrived: how many, and a link, never what was written. Turn it off in your account menu › Email, or with the link at the bottom of the email. Every suggestion from a player gets the same four choices:

Accept
It shows on the page exactly as they wrote it.
Edit
Change the wording first, then accept.
Ask Claude to work it in
On a person, place or other entry: Claude writes a new description that includes it. It waits under Claude's drafts to check until you use, edit or discard it. On a session: Claude redrafts the recap with it, and you review and publish again.
Dismiss
Drop it. Nothing on the page changes.
  • On a recap: Edit the recap yourself and press I fixed it, or Ask Claude to redraft with it. (Sessions you wrote by hand can't be redrafted, so edit those. One from a recording can be, while askdnd keeps its transcript.)
  • On a glossary term: Edit the term (or Add the term for a new one) right there, and it's accepted when you save.
  • On an entry (on the Campaign page, or its side panel elsewhere) you can also Accept or Dismiss without opening the inbox. When you've worked a fact into the description yourself, press Done to take it off What the table knows.
  • Players see what happened to theirs, in the entry's panel and on Your notes (in their account menu): Added (on the page), Used (worked into a description or recap), or Not used. With any decision you can write a word to them (the line above the buttons in the Inbox: why, or what you changed); it's never required, and they read it under What happened. Your decision shows them a red count on their picture until they look, and a corner card once.
  • Your own suggestions skip all of this and go straight on.

The inbox also lists sessions to review; each still has its own page with the recap, key moments, questions and suggested updates.

Writing it yourself

  • A new person, place, faction, item, quest or creature: + New entry on the Campaign page, or + New quest on its Quests tab. Prepare an NPC before the party meets them by ticking Hide from the table for now: until you show it, it's as if it doesn't exist. The table never sees it, its name never shows on a key moment, and Claude isn't told about it when drafting recaps, questions or descriptions.
  • Change an entry: pick it on the Campaign page (or click its name anywhere), then Edit. You can rename it (the old name keeps working), write the description in your own words, add relationships, a picture or a key moment, or hide it.
  • Pictures: an entry with no picture shows its place on the Campaign page, in the shape for its kind (a portrait for a person, a wide banner for a place, an emblem for a faction), with ✦ Draw one (the Picture window on Describe it, the entry's description already filled in) and Upload; players see no empty box, only a badge with its initials. Add a picture in Edit opens the same window: upload one, or Describe it and pick one of four drawn in your campaign's Art style (Manage › Settings: a few words and up to four pictures to match, the campaign's own or its characters' D&D Beyond portraits; switch Describe it off there if you'd rather not). Describe it can also go by other pictures, sent along only when ticked: a relative's (listed under Pictures to go by, so a family looks related) and any entry you name with @ that has a picture (the Leviathan, to draw Crow's Keep on its mast). Drawn pictures count toward the monthly spending limit. The last step is Frame it: drag the picture under the square and zoom, and every place shows the part around its middle (the list's square, the entry's own shape, in the side panel too: only a place's picture is wide); Frame it in the Picture window frames one already there.
  • Quests: each open quest on the Quests tab has Edit and Mark done; a done quest moves to Done (to reopen it, set it back to Open in Edit). To show who gave a quest, add the relationship gave on that person (or given by on the quest). The people and places on a quest's card come from its key moments, so tagging the quest in a key moment keeps the card current.
  • Relationships (parent of, married to, sibling of, member of, serves, ally of, enemy of, gave) are added in an entry's Edit window. The Relationships tab draws them as family trees and lists, and each entry in World shows its connections around it.
  • Familiars and steeds make their own entries: the first time a player casts Find Familiar or Find Steed in askdnd (where Your creatures is on), it becomes a Creature entry the table can see, serving its master, named as the player named it (or using an entry you already wrote with that name). askdnd keeps its forms, where it is and when it disappeared on the entry; you can edit it like any other, and recaps add its key moments. Summons don't get one.
  • Hidden entries show in your list with a Hidden tag; the table doesn't see them until you tick The table can see it in Edit.
  • Claude's help, when you want it: in an entry's Edit window, Regenerate description rewrites it from the facts and key moments; you see it before anything changes. Facts it worked in come off the list when you accept.
  • A session with no notes or recording: Sessions › + Add session, write what happened, and choose Save as the recap (your words, no Claude) or Save and ask Claude (Claude checks it against the sessions around it and asks questions).
  • A spelling or term: Campaign › Glossary › Add a term. Transcripts use the glossary to spell names right.

House rules

  • Your rulings on how askdnd rolls things go on Manage › House rules: one roll for all of Magic Missile's darts, or a short ruling on any spell ("Fireball: I decide who is in the area"). The roll window shows House rule and your words to whoever rolls it.
  • Your corrections to items start from the item: open it from a character's page and use Correct this at the bottom of its side panel, for what it costs, whether it's on Battle, or to hide it. They show here. Use one for homebrew or anything your table does differently: askdnd takes your campaign's correction first, then its own, then the rules, then the item's description.
  • An item that brings a creature (a homebrew bone that becomes a beast, a figurine): in its side panel, It brings a creature above Correct this is filled in from the item's words (what using it costs, its range, the creature's type and immunities, how it's commanded, how long it lasts, when it comes back). Check each part and pick the creature from your books or the SRD, since the words rarely say which. Then its player's Battle has it: Use brings the creature with its own Battle and Hit Points (askdnd's, not on D&D Beyond), Command it sits in their column, and at 0 Hit Points or Dismiss it's spent until the rest you chose. It's listed here with your other corrections.
  • They apply to this campaign only and never change askdnd's own rules data. Remove one and the spell rolls as the 2024 rules say again; History can bring it back.

Your words are kept

askdnd remembers who wrote each description and recap last. Once you've written or edited one, it's yours:

  • Claude keeps your wording. When a later session, a redraft or Ask Claude to work it in changes something you wrote, Claude is told to keep your words and only add or fix what's new.
  • You see every change marked up before anything happens: struck through means it would go, highlighted means Claude added it. Accept, Edit or Reject as usual.
  • Nothing overwrites a newer edit. If you changed a description after Claude drafted its update, accepting the old update is refused; use Edit to combine the two.
  • Recaps: if Claude redrafts a recap you edited, the session's page shows what it changed, with Keep your version to go straight back to yours.
  • An entry's Edit window says who wrote its description (Written by …).

Tidying and undo

  • Merge a duplicate into the entry to keep (Edit › Merge or delete): its name becomes another name there, and its facts, relationships, key moments and picture move over.
  • Hide an entry (Edit › Details › untick The table can see it) to keep it from the table without losing it.
  • Delete an entry you don't need (Edit › Merge or delete). Player characters can't be deleted here.
  • Undo anything on Manage › History: every name, description, fact, relationship and key moment change, with Restore.

Who sees what

  • Everything published is visible to everyone at the table.
  • Drafts, hidden entries and these Manage pages are only for you, reviewers and admins.
  • Your scene notes on askdnd's table are only yours: not reviewers', not admins', and never Claude's. Creatures you hide on its map reach no player's screen.
  • As DM you never see players' own notes, pins, shortlists or reminders. That's on purpose: they can plan without spoilers going the other way.
  • Being DM is a role in this campaign (you can see it on Manage › Table), and it includes everything a reviewer can do here. If you ever play in another campaign, you'd be a player there.
