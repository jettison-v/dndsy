The idea: you own what the table reads

Recaps, key moments, people, places, factions, items, quests and the glossary are the campaign record. Three things feed it, and nothing from Claude or a player reaches the table until you (or a reviewer) say yes:

Brett's notes and recordings→ Claude drafts →You review→The table
Players' suggestions→You decide→The table
What you write→ straight to →The table

Nothing is ever lost: every change is kept on Manage › History with Restore.

Tips
  • Go in order. Each draft builds on the recaps before it, so earlier fixes make later drafts better.
  • Small bites work. One or two sessions before each game night clears it in a few weeks.
  • Good enough is fine. Everything stays editable, and History keeps every earlier version.

After a game night (once the backlog is done)

  1. Brett records the session; the Processor on his Mac removes table talk and sends only the game to askdnd.
  2. A draft appears on Recaps within a few minutes.
  3. Review and publish it, as above. Before the next session is ideal, so the table's Previously on… is ready.

When a player suggests something

  • On a person, place or other entry, it shows in that entry's panel under What the table knows, marked waiting. Merge shows it to the table; Reject drops it. When you've worked a fact into the description yourself, press Done to take it off the list.
  • On a recap or a glossary term, it goes to Manage › Suggestions. Fix the recap or term yourself, or Use it so the next redraft includes it.
  • Your own suggestions skip all of this and go straight on.

Coming soon: one inbox for every suggestion, with the same buttons everywhere (Accept, Edit, Ask Claude to work it in, Dismiss), and players will see what happened to theirs.

Writing it yourself

  • A new person, place, faction, item, quest or creature: + New entry on the Campaign page (or + New in a tab). Prepare an NPC before the party meets them by ticking Hide from the table for now.
  • Change an entry: click it, then Edit. You can rename it (the old name keeps working), write the description in your own words, add relationships, a picture or a key moment, mark a quest done, or hide it.
  • Claude's help, when you want it: in an entry's Edit window, Regenerate description rewrites it from the facts and key moments; you see it before anything changes. Facts it worked in come off the list when you accept.
  • A session with no notes or recording: Recaps › + Add session, write what happened, and choose Save as the recap (your words, no Claude) or Save and ask Claude (Claude checks it against the sessions around it and asks questions).
  • A spelling or term: Campaign › Glossary › Add a term. Transcripts use the glossary to spell names right.

Tidying and undo

  • Merge a duplicate into the entry to keep (Edit › Merge or delete): its name becomes another name there, and its facts, relationships, key moments and picture move over.
  • Hide an entry (Edit › Details › untick The table can see it) to keep it from the table without losing it.
  • Delete an entry you don't need (Edit › Merge or delete). Player characters can't be deleted here.
  • Undo anything on Manage › History: every name, description, fact, relationship and key moment change, with Restore.

Who sees what

  • Everything published is visible to everyone at the table.
  • Drafts, hidden entries and these Manage pages are only for you, reviewers and admins.
  • As DM you never see players' own notes, pins, shortlists or reminders. That's on purpose: they can plan without spoilers going the other way.

Words you'll see

Draft
Written (by Claude or you) but not shown to the table yet.
Publish
Show a session's recap and key moments to the table.
Hidden
An entry the table can't see.
Key moment
One line about something important in a session; shown on the session and on every entry it mentions.
What the table knows
Facts added by players or you, listed on an entry until worked into its description.
Suggested update
A change Claude proposes while drafting a session (a new person, a spelling, a new description).
Claude's questions
Things Claude wasn't sure about while drafting; answering them improves this and later drafts.
Redraft
Claude drafts the session again, using your answers and corrections.
