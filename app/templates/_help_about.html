What it's for

  • Remembering the story. Story recaps each session (Previously on… for the latest, All sessions for every one), and Campaign keeps what the party has met: World (the people, places, factions, items and creatures, each one to read on the page, with a dot on the ones from the last session), Quests (what the party is doing and where each quest stands), Relationships (family trees and who's tied to whom) the Glossary and the Map of the Sword Coast (places on it have a Show on map button).
  • Knowing your character. Each character's page (Battle, Sheet and Plan) and Party read each character from D&D Beyond and lay out what you can do, sorted by action, bonus action and reaction, so nothing gets forgotten. Saves show on Battle, and an Adv badge marks anything you have Advantage on (hover it for when). Druids see each Wild Shape form with their own feats, class features and proficiencies carried over.
  • Rules answers with book pages are coming later. Until then they stay on the older askdnd at askdnd.ai.

Ground rules

  • It informs; it doesn't advise. It explains what you have and how it works. It never tells you what to do.
  • The DM makes every ruling. Rule explanations here help the table talk it through; they don't decide anything. The DM's house rules (Manage › House rules) change how askdnd rolls things at this table only.
  • 2024 core rules only: the Player's Handbook, Dungeon Master's Guide and Monster Manual. Content from other books is left out or labeled.
  • Each campaign is its own table. Its story, house rules and roles are kept apart from any other campaign in askdnd; you only see the campaigns you're in.
  • The DM controls the story. The DM's corrections go straight in. Everyone else's suggestions go to the DM and the admins first.
  • Running a campaign happens inside it. The DM's Manage pages (what needs them, the inbox, sessions, who plays, house rules, history, settings) belong to their campaign. Things that are the same for every campaign (accounts, spending, backups) are for admins.

Where the information comes from

  • Sessions. Sessions are recorded and transcribed. A filter on the admin's own computer removes off-topic talk before anything leaves it. Only the game content goes to Claude (Anthropic's AI), which drafts the recap. A person reviews every recap before the table sees it. Raw transcripts are never uploaded or stored.
  • Characters. askdnd reads each character's public D&D Beyond page and keeps the last good copy. It can't change anything on D&D Beyond or roll dice there.
  • Table rolls. With Game Mode on, askdnd's server rolls your character's dice and keeps each roll (who, what, the dice, any bonuses, its damage or reroll, and who may see it) so the table can see it. With Game Mode off, rolls stay in your browser.
  • The table. Anyone can suggest a correction or addition from any page. Rule-term definitions are askdnd's own short summaries of the 2024 rules.

Privacy, briefly

  • Only people on the invite list can sign in, with Google.
  • Character notes (each player's notes on why they built their character this way) are never shown to the DM.
  • Your pins, Shortlist, Reminders and notes on your own character are yours: only you can set them, and the DM never sees them.
  • Table rolls go to everyone, to the DM only (others see only that you rolled), or to you alone, as you choose for each roll.
  • Each character's background and backstory from D&D Beyond (personality, ideals, bonds, flaws, appearance) and players' item notes are visible to everyone at the table, the DM included. They're useful context for the whole campaign.
  • Campaign data lives in askdnd's database, not in its code or on GitHub.
  • Claude use has a monthly spending limit.

askdnd isn't affiliated with Wizards of the Coast or D&D Beyond.
