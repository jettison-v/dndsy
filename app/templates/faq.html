FAQ

How askdnd's features work, so nothing comes as a surprise. The short version is on the About page.

The story

{% call q("Where do the recaps come from?") %}

Sessions are recorded and transcribed. A filter on the admin's computer removes off-topic talk first, and only the game content goes to Claude, which drafts a recap with key moments. A reviewer reads, edits and publishes it. You only see published recaps, on Previously on… (latest) and Sessions (all of them).

{% endcall %} {% call q("How does the Campaign page work?", "campaign") %}

It has four tabs. World is everything the party has met, to read: names down the left, grouped into People, Places, Factions, Items and Creatures, and the one you pick on the right with its description, the quests it's part of, its connections (everyone it's tied to, drawn around it: parents above, spouse and siblings beside, children below, allies and enemies on one side, factions and quests on the other; click a name to read about them), what the table knows, and a timeline of its key moments. A blue dot marks names that came up in the last session's key moments, and Last session (above the names) shows only those. The search looks through names, other names and descriptions, and the ↑ and ↓ keys move through the names.

Quests shows every open quest at once: who gave it and since when, its last few key moments (earlier opens the rest), who and where it involves, and what the table knows. If an open quest hasn't been in a key moment for three sessions, it says so. Finished quests are listed under Done; click one for everything about it. Relationships draws a small tree for each family (couples, then their children) and lists the other ties: factions and their members, who serves whom, allies, enemies and who gave which quest. Click anyone there to see their connections; a name in that window puts them in the middle.

Each entry and tab has its own address, so you can share a link to it. Elsewhere (a recap, a key moment), clicking a name still opens a quick side panel, with Open in Campaign for the full entry.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Something in a recap or on a Campaign page is wrong. What do I do?") %}

Use Suggest a correction or addition under the recap, or on any person, place or other entry (on the Campaign page, or in its side panel when you click its name elsewhere), or in a glossary term's panel. Write what's wrong or what to add; it shows in the panel right away. Every suggestion helps: corrections also teach future drafts.

The DM, reviewers and admins have Edit on each entry, and Mark done on a quest on the Quests tab. Edit is a large window to change the name, kind or quest status, hide an entry from the table, write the description in their own words (or have Claude redraft it from the facts), manage relationships (including who gave a quest) and pictures, add a key moment, merge a duplicate into the entry to keep, or delete it (Manage › History can bring it back). They also add new people, places and other entries with + New entry (or + New quest) on the Campaign page, and can write a session's recap by hand on Manage › Recaps › Add session. Manage › DM guide walks the DM through all of it. When Claude's redraft works a fact in, accepting it moves that fact under Done; they can also mark any fact Done by hand. Undo brings it back.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Who sees my suggestion, and when does it change things?") %}

It goes to the DM and the admins (and any reviewers) on Manage › Inbox. Nothing changes until one of them decides: accept it as you wrote it, edit it first, ask Claude to work it into the description or recap, or dismiss it.

Once the DM (or anyone) writes or edits a description or recap, Claude keeps those words: later updates from a session or a redraft only add or fix what's new, and the DM sees each change marked up before accepting it.

You can see what happened to each of yours on Your suggestions (in the account menu, or under More on a phone), and on the entry's side panel: Waiting, Added (on the page as you wrote it), Used (worked into a description or recap) or Not used. There are no notifications.

The DM's own suggestions skip this step, because the DM controls the story. A DM fact goes straight onto the page, and a DM correction to a session shows under the recap as "From the DM" right away.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What are \"Questions for you\" on Previously on…?") %}

When Claude isn't sure about something while drafting ("who told the party about the library?"), a reviewer can send the question to the person most likely to know. Your answer goes into the next draft. You can also tick that it should be used in every future draft, which helps with things like name spellings.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What's the glossary?") %}

Campaign names and words with their correct spelling, plus how a transcript tends to mishear them. It keeps names consistent in recaps and links. The DM and reviewers can edit it directly; anyone can suggest a term.

{% endcall %}

Characters

{% call q("Where does my character come from?") %}

From your character's page on D&D Beyond. It has to be set to Public there (Character Settings › Character Privacy), and an admin links it on the campaign's Manage › Table. askdnd keeps the last good copy, so if D&D Beyond is down you still see your character, with the time it was last updated.

D&D Beyond doesn't officially offer this data, so if they change their format, refreshing may stop working for a while. The numbers have been checked against D&D Beyond's own sheet; if one ever looks different, tell an admin.

{% endcall %} {% call q("How do I get the latest from D&D Beyond?") %}

Use Live in the menu bar (the pill that says when the copy was last updated; it turns green while Game Mode is on). Open it to turn on Game Mode: the character you're looking at (or your own) refreshes every {{ "%g" | format(game_mode_minutes) }} minutes while askdnd is open, and on Battle the page updates itself. Game Mode is remembered per browser. The same window lists when each character was last refreshed (and any that couldn't be), and has Refresh <character> now for anyone; reviewers also get Refresh everyone.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Can askdnd roll dice or change things on D&D Beyond?") %}

No. It only reads your public character. D&D Beyond's terms don't allow other apps to act on it, so rolls, HP and spell slots are still done in D&D Beyond.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What are Battle, Sheet, Plan and Party?", "modes") %}

Each character has one page with three modes, switched at the top right of the page: Battle, Sheet and Plan. Your character's name in the menu bar (for example Cole ▾) switches characters and keeps the mode you're in. On a phone, the bar at the bottom has Recap, Campaign, Battle, Party and More.

  • Battle: your plan for a fight, grouped by action, bonus action and reaction: the columns show only what you pinned (+ Add in each column's heading lists what you haven't pinned yet, and See all under it lists everything you could do), plus all six saving throws (● proficient), your Reminders and your Battle combos. The DM, who can't see pins, sees the usual options instead. The whole party is on Party. Rules (n) at the top right of a column lists class and feat rules that change it (hover or tap). The Action heading says how many attacks your Attack action makes (Extra Attack), and On a hit lists extras you can add when an attack hits, such as Sneak Attack, Stunning Strike or Savage Attacker, with what they cost. Weapon masteries show only on weapons you've mastered. Anything that uses something up says what and how many are left: "1 Focus Point ●●", "1st-level ●●●●", "Free cast ●". Your limited resources (Focus Points, Luck Points…) sit above the numbers, in Wild Shape too, and spell slots have their own line. The short help line on Battle, Sheet and Plan has More (the answer here) and Got it, which hides it for you on every device. On a phone, Action, Bonus and Reaction are tabs.
  • Sheet: the full sheet in tabs: Actions, Spells (Prepared, your Shortlist, or All you could prepare, with the same filters as the Spell Book), Wild Shape (for druids), Inventory (Equipped, your Shortlist or All, filtered by kind, magic, use, attunement and rarity), Features (All or your Shortlist, filtered by where they come from, what they are used as and limited uses), Skills & situational rules, and Background (personality, ideals, bonds, flaws, appearance and backstory, from D&D Beyond).
  • Plan: your own planning, in tabs: Battle combos, Leveling strategy, Personal quests, General notes, Reminders, and Spells and Wild Shapes, each with your Shortlist (to compare) and All (to pick from), with the same search, filters and sorting as Sheet (Full notes shows your notes on spells and forms in full instead of one line). Players only: the DM never sees Plan.
  • Party: everyone's key numbers and resources, then the party's spells and items pooled together in two tabs, grouped by what they do (jump to a group from the links at the top). Click a name for a short version of that character.

Click any row to open the side panel with everything about it: costs, numbers, what it does, and the full text. From a list in the panel (like "See all"), ← Back returns to the list.

The number tiles at the top (HP, AC, Speed, Initiative, Spell DC, Heroic Inspiration…) open too: each explains what the number means and how it adds up, naming the feature or item behind every part.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What do the pin, the bookmark, the bell and the note do?", "marks") %}

The pin puts something you do on your turn (an action, bonus action or reaction) on Battle, and also adds it to your Shortlist. The bookmark adds a spell or Wild Shape form to your Shortlist on Plan without preparing or learning it (more kinds of things will follow as each list gets a Shortlist view). The bell adds it to your Reminders on Battle and Plan (for example, Advantage against being Charmed); you can also write a reminder of your own in the Reminders box ("Ask the DM about the map"). Each thing can have one note, written in its side panel ("save for the sea leg", "only when concentrating"); it shows under that thing wherever it appears and in full in the side panel. Only your character's player can set these; not admins, and the DM never sees them.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What are player notes?", "notes") %}

Plan on your character's page holds your own notes: Battle combos (a setup turn and a payoff turn, each split into action, bonus action, reaction and movement), a Leveling strategy (level by level, with what you'll take and why), Personal quests (goal, why, who's involved and the leads) and free-form General notes (with buttons for bold, italics, headings and lists, so no formatting codes to learn). Spells, features, items, Wild Shape forms, people and places you mention become links. Your combos also show on Battle, where you can add one too. On Plan each kind is a compact list (notes are tiles); click one for the whole thing (combos and leveling plans in a large window, the rest in the side panel), drag to put them in your own order, and saving keeps you on the tab you were on.

Only your character's player can write them. Every player can read them; the DM never sees them.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What does the Adv badge mean?") %}

A small Adv badge next to a save, skill, Initiative or attack means something gives you Advantage on it. Rest the mouse on the badge for when it applies and where it comes from (a species trait, class feature, feat, item, or a Wild Shape form's trait such as Pack Tactics). A dashed outline means it only applies sometimes, such as War Caster's "to maintain Concentration". It's the same badge on Battle, Sheet, Party and the Wild Shape cards. Advantage that applies to every save (for example "against being Charmed") is said once under the saves.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What is the Spell Book?", "spell-book") %}

On Battle, characters with spells have a spell bar under the header: the Spell Book button, your spell slots, your pinned spells (the four you used most recently; click one to roll it) and what you're concentrating on.

The Spell Book lists the spells you have prepared, one per row, with your note on a spell under its name. Filters on the left narrow it down: Level, Effect (Damage, Heal, Control, Support, Utility), Casting time and Concentration, plus More filters (ritual, attack or save, what a successful save does, area, damage type, range). Within a group a spell can match any chip you pick; across groups it must match all of them. Each chip shows how many spells it would leave; click it again to turn it off. Searching looks through every spell and pauses the filters. Your filters are remembered in this browser for a day. Save spells say what a successful save does (half damage, nothing, or damage that needs no save). The Damage / Healing column shows each spell's average (healing in green); Cast at shows them at a higher slot level, for upcasting, and you can sort by Amount. Click a spell for its side panel: the full text, your note, and Pin, Shortlist and Remind me. Averages are just the dice's average: they inform, they don't recommend.

Concentrate on a spell to track it: the bar shows the spell, its level and the round (press +1 each round). Save opens the Concentration save: enter the damage you just took and it works out the DC (10, or half the damage if higher), with your Con bonus and any Advantage (War Caster). ✕ asks before ending it. Spend the spell slot on D&D Beyond as usual; askdnd reads it on the next refresh.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Do magic items work on Battle?", "items") %}

Items that give you an action show under + Add and See all in the right column on Battle, ready to pin: a Ring of Invisibility offers Turn Invisible (a Magic action) and Become visible (a Bonus Action). Pressing Turn Invisible puts an Invisible pill in the Status row under Battle's header, which everyone at the table sees; a potion's option has a Used it: button for what it starts (Enlarged, Water Breathing, Invisible). + Condition adds any of the 15 conditions (Exhaustion by level) or an effect. Open a pill for what it does and to end it. The roll window lists what each one changes for that roll (Advantage, Disadvantage, an automatic failure, or Exhaustion's −2 per level) without choosing for you: a creature that can see you cancels Invisible's Advantage, and the DM decides. An item that needs attunement works only once you're attuned; its side panel says how many of your 3 attunement slots are in use.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Can I roll dice in askdnd?") %}

Yes. Click anything that rolls: a to-hit number (the boxed +7), a save, Initiative's Roll, a spell's or item's Roll or Heal (Potion of Healing, Second Wind…), and on Sheet's Skills tab any skill or ability bonus. The same buttons are in the side panel (an option's details and "See all"). Features that make Unarmed Strikes, like Flurry of Blows, roll the Unarmed Strike. A small roll window opens next to it (at the bottom on phones):

  • Normal, Advantage or Disadvantage. Advantage that always applies is already chosen, and the window lists what gives you Advantage and when, plus Lucky's Luck Points and Heroic Inspiration. The DM decides.
  • Add dice or a bonus (folded): click dice to add them (− then a die subtracts one, like Bane) and type any bonus.
  • Who sees it (with Game Mode on).

Spells, Wild Shape attacks and items roll exactly what the rules say. askdnd keeps a checked "recipe" for every spell in the 2024 rules' free reference (the SRD), the Player's Handbook spells the table uses, the Beasts and common items: every damage part with its type (the Giant Toad's bite is piercing plus poison), each dart or ray on its own (Magic Missile's darts, a separate attack roll for each of Scorching Ray's rays), and choices where the rules give one (Toll the Dead on an unhurt or hurt target, a Rhinoceros's charge, a Bloodied swarm). For a spell that gets stronger with a higher slot, choose Cast at level in the roll window (levels you have no slot left for are marked); cantrips grow with your level by themselves. Healing spells add your spellcasting modifier only when the spell says so. A spell from another book uses D&D Beyond's numbers, and the window says they weren't checked. The full audit is in docs/rules/rolls-audit.md.

House rules. The DM can change how a spell rolls at this table (for example, one roll for all of Magic Missile's darts) or add a short ruling to it, on Manage › House rules. The roll window then says House rule and what it is. House rules apply to this campaign only; askdnd's own recipes don't change.

Press Roll (or Enter; Shift-click a roll button to skip the window). The window turns into the result after a short dice tumble (click to skip). From there you can roll damage after an attack (every die doubled on a natural 20, added dice too), reroll with Heroic Inspiration (or a 1 with a Halfling's Luck), or add a die such as Bardic Inspiration. They stay attached to the roll.

With Game Mode on (the Live control in the menu bar), rolls for your own character are shared: askdnd's server rolls the dice and everyone sees them in Table rolls, a sidebar on the left of every page (a sheet at the bottom on phones). It's open on Battle and a narrow strip elsewhere; « collapses or opens it, and your choice is remembered on that device. At its bottom, Checks, skills, saves, Death save, Hit Die rolls for your character, and Manual roll is for when the DM asks for "2d6" (click d6 twice). Collapsed, it shows one picture per person with their latest roll (green for a natural 20, red for a 1); a new roll makes that picture pulse, and resting the mouse on it (or tapping it) says what the roll was. After a few minutes with no rolls the collapsed strip slides out of the way and comes back with the next roll; the small tab at the edge brings it back any time. While it's collapsed or hidden, other players' rolls arrive as small notices. A roll says sending… until askdnd has it; if it can't get through, it says so and offers Try again. The dice animation can be turned on or off there for your device.

With Game Mode off, or on someone else's character, rolls are private: they're rolled in your browser and nobody else sees them. Neither kind goes to D&D Beyond's Game Log.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Who sees my table rolls?") %}

Choose under Who sees in the roll window (it starts at Table each time you open askdnd):

  • Table: everyone.
  • DM only: you and the DM see the number. Everyone else sees only "Cole rolled for the DM": not what it was for, the dice or the total. The DM can roll dice the same way: the table sees "rolled behind the screen".
  • Just me: only you, not even the DM.

Damage, rerolls and added dice go to the same people as the roll they belong to. The server decides who gets what, so a hidden number never reaches anyone else's browser. Only a character's own player can roll for it at the table. The DM still makes every ruling.

{% endcall %} {% call q("How does Sheet's Wild Shape tab work?", "wild-shape") %}

For druids, it lists the Beasts you could learn now (from the free 2024 rules, with D&D Beyond's pictures), filtered by your Druid level: the Challenge Rating limit (higher with Circle of the Moon), and no Fly Speed before level 8. Each form is a row with its picture, CR, AC, Temporary Hit Points, attack bonus, damage per turn and speeds, with the same kind of filters as the spell lists (status, Challenge Rating, how it moves, size). Known shows the forms you've picked on D&D Beyond and All you can learn everything else; the bookmark adds a form to your Shortlist on Plan, for comparing. Click a form to see it as you: the Beast's Strength, Dexterity, Constitution and attacks with your Hit Points, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma and proficiencies, plus Circle of the Moon's AC, Temporary Hit Points and Radiant option, laid out as battle cards. Your feats and class features (from every class) carry over and are counted: for example Alert adds to Initiative, and War Caster's Advantage shows on the Constitution save, but only for keeping Concentration. Species traits (Darkvision, Fey Ancestry…) don't carry into a form. You keep your languages and can speak in a form (a 2024 change). Unarmored Movement, when kept, adds to every speed the form has. Hover any number for how it adds up.

Your DM may let you keep some things in a form. Tick them under Kept in Wild Shape in any form's window, in two groups: Traits (class traits such as Unarmored Defense and Unarmored Movement) and Equipment (worn items such as a Luckstone or Insignia of Claws). The numbers update for every form, and the choice is saved until you change it.

On Battle, the Wild Shape buttons switch the page to a known form until you press Normal. Everyone at the table sees which form you're in. askdnd informs; the DM makes the rulings (for example, which of your other features work in a form).

{% endcall %} {% call q("What are the dotted underlines in descriptions?") %}

Rule terms such as Unarmed Strike, Prone or Abyssal. Rest the mouse on one, or tap it, for a short definition. Only the first mention in a panel is underlined, and terms everyone knows (Advantage, Long Rest…) aren't. The definitions are askdnd's own short summaries of the 2024 rules; the DM makes the call at the table.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What do the Custom, Changed and Note tags on items mean?") %}
  • Custom: made on D&D Beyond for this campaign, not from a book. Its description is whatever was typed there.
  • Changed: someone renamed it, added a note, or changed details such as cost on D&D Beyond. The side panel says what.
  • Note: the player wrote a note on it. Open the item to read it.
{% endcall %} {% call q("Why isn't some content from my D&D Beyond sheet here?") %}

The table plays the 2024 core rules only, so options from other books (Forgotten Realms extras, for example) are left out, and anything non-core already on a sheet is labeled. An admin can also move or hide things that D&D Beyond groups oddly, for every character at once.

{% endcall %}

Behind the scenes

{% call q("How is askdnd's information organized?", "data") %}

Four kinds of information, each kept in its own place:

The rules knowledge is the same for everyone and changes only when askdnd is updated. The campaign holds what happened at the table, who plays which role there (DM, reviewer, player) and the DM's house rules. A character holds what's about the character: its D&D Beyond copy and its player's own marks and notes, which the DM never sees. A character joins a campaign on a date and could later move to another, while what it did stays in the first one's story.

Changes are layered, most specific last: askdnd's rules knowledge, then an admin's tool correction (fixing how askdnd reads D&D Beyond, for everyone), then the DM's house rule (for this table only). Removing a correction or a house rule brings back what's underneath.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Can askdnd hold more than one campaign?", "campaigns") %}

Yes. If you're in more than one, the campaign's name shows next to askdnd in the menu bar (under More on phones); pick another to switch. askdnd remembers the one you were in last. Story pages carry the campaign in their address, like /c/main/sessions, so a link you share opens the right campaign; old links open the campaign you're in.

Each campaign has its own recaps, Campaign pages, map, glossary, table rolls, house rules and roles: you can be the DM of one and a player in another, and you only see campaigns you have a role in (admins see them all). A character is in one campaign at a time, like on D&D Beyond. If it moves, what it did stays in its old campaign's story; its D&D Beyond copy, notes, pins and reminders go with it, and personal quests stay with the campaign they were written in. Admins set campaigns up on Manage › Campaigns. Each campaign's own Manage pages (its inbox, sessions, table, house rules, history and settings) are at its address too, like /c/main/manage, so they always act on that campaign.

{% endcall %}

People, pictures and privacy

{% call q("Who can see what?", "privacy") %}
  • Everything for the table (recaps, Campaign, characters, each character's D&D Beyond background and backstory, item notes) is visible to everyone signed in, the DM included.
  • Character notes (a player's notes on why they built their character this way) are never shown to the DM.
  • Pins, the Shortlist, Reminders and notes belong to the character's player and are hidden from the DM.
  • Pages that aren't published yet are only visible to reviewers.
{% endcall %} {% call q("What can players, reviewers, admins and the DM do?") %}
  • Player: read everything for the table and suggest corrections.
  • Reviewer: also drafts, edits and publishes sessions, decides on suggestions, refreshes characters and adds pictures.
  • DM: everything a reviewer does; their own corrections skip review, they decide on everyone else's suggestions, edit campaign entries (names, descriptions, relationships), edit the glossary and add pictures. The DM never sees the character notes, pins, shortlists, reminders or notes on things of the characters in the campaign they run.
  • Admin: also manages accounts, roles, character links and regrouping, and sees usage and costs. An admin who is also the DM still never sees those notes.

Player, Reviewer and DM are roles in a campaign, so someone can run one campaign and play in another. Admin is about askdnd as a whole. Admins set who can sign in (and who's an admin) on Manage › Accounts, and each campaign's roles and characters on its Manage › Table. The DM can see their campaign's Table and change its name and their own name (as Claude writes it in recaps) on Manage › Settings.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Where do the pictures come from?") %}

Player characters show their D&D Beyond portrait. The DM, reviewers and admins can add a picture to any person, place, faction or other entry from its page (Add a picture). Uploads are shrunk, and hidden information such as a phone photo's location is removed.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Are session recordings or transcripts stored?") %}

No. Transcripts are filtered on the admin's own computer, only game content is sent to Claude, and only the extracted story (recaps, key moments, the campaign record) is kept.

{% endcall %} {% call q("What if something gets changed or deleted by mistake?") %}

askdnd keeps a history of every change to the campaign record (entries, other names, relationships, facts and key moments), the glossary, sessions, characters, people and players' notes: what it was, what it became, who changed it and how. Reviewers and admins can open the campaign's Manage › History and press Restore to put an earlier version back (deleted notes and glossary terms can be brought back too). Each entry also links to its own history. Players' notes stay hidden from the DM there as well. Changes to accounts, which belong to no campaign, are on the admins' own History.

Imports on Manage › Backups and imports show a preview before anything is saved, and a whole import can be undone afterwards.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Is askdnd backed up?") %}

Yes. Every night (3 am Pacific) the whole database is copied to a private Amazon S3 storage bucket, and the last 30 days of copies are kept. That copy lives outside Railway, where askdnd runs, so even a problem there wouldn't lose the campaign. The backup job has its own key that can only reach its backups folder. Restoring one is a deliberate step the admin takes with the restore script; it's been tested. If a night's backup fails or doesn't run, it shows on Manage › Needs you for admins.

{% endcall %} {% call q("Does using askdnd cost anything?") %}

Not for players. Claude is paid per use by the admin, with a monthly limit: drafting a recap costs a few cents, and regenerating a Campaign description about 1–2¢. Admins see spending on Manage › Usage and can set askdnd's own monthly limit there: once a month's spending reaches it, askdnd pauses drafts and description rewrites until the 1st (or until the limit is raised) and says so.

{% endcall %} {% call q("How do I get someone signed in?") %}

An admin adds their Google email on Manage › Accounts (or straight to a campaign on its Manage › Table). While the Google sign-in is in testing, the admin also adds them as a test user in Google Cloud Console.
