Cole's Spells tab, editing

Change spells on the Druid card turns the tab into edit mode. The card becomes the edit bar, which stays at the top as you scroll: the reason, a rule line for each list, and what you've changed. Nothing in the list goes: every column, filter and the search stay. Only the bookmark and bell make way for one button, Prepare or Unprepare (Learn or Let go for a cantrip). Cole has 10 of 10 prepared, so Prepare is greyed until you unprepare one, as on D&D Beyond. Try swapping a cantrip after a Long Rest.

From the Long Rest window and the level-up cardTheir Prepare spells button opens Sheet › Spells already in edit mode, the reason set ("After a Long Rest", "I gained a Druid level"). From the tab itself it starts at Just looking.
One Make, one UndoNothing reaches D&D Beyond until Make, so a half-done swap never shows on the sheet; the corner card's Undo puts it all back. Cancel leaves edit mode with nothing changed.
The views in edit modeThe list opens on All (what you could take), not Prepared. "Yours" shows what you'd end with, changes included.
C · Two classes

Big C's Spells tab: the class cards become the tabs

The Paladin and Sorcerer cards already sit on top of his Spells tab. In edit mode they are the class tabs you picked. Each holds its own rule lines, and the list shows only that class's spells, so a Paladin rule is never read beside a Sorcerer spell. After a Long Rest the Paladin card says Now and the Sorcerer card Not now. Open the Sorcerer tab and swap a cantrip to see the warning.

C · Wild Shape

Cole's Wild Shape tab, editing

The same edit mode on Sheet › Wild Shape: Change forms on the Wild Shape card, the edit bar with its rule line, and Learn or Let go where the bookmark and bell are. The list keeps AC, Temporary Hit Points, to hit, damage a turn and Speed, which is what you choose a form by. A Beast he can't take yet shows why where its button would be.

D · A wider modal

The same list in a window

For changes made from somewhere else (the Long Rest window, a level-up card on Battle) without leaving that page. The window is the tab's own list at full width, with every column, filters and search. The rules sit in its header, and Your changes and Make sit in a tray along the bottom, not in a side column, so the list keeps its width. It's the same pieces as C, so the two can be the same code: C on Sheet, D wherever Sheet isn't open.

D&D Beyond enforces none of these; it shows Learn and Delete at any time. askdnd holds the counts as D&D Beyond does (no more prepared or known than your number) and never limits a swap: it says each rule where you make the change, marks what applies now, and warns about a swap past it. Your picks

Three things to decide

Where the change happens: C (in the tab), D (a wider window), or both?

My pick is both, sharing one list: C on Sheet › Spells and Sheet › Wild Shape; D only where Sheet isn't open (the Long Rest window and Battle's level-up card). If you want one place only, C, with those buttons going to Sheet in edit mode.

The button where the bookmark and bell are, or the tick column on the left?

My pick is your idea, drawn here: a labeled button on the right reads clearly (Prepare, Unprepare, Learn, Let go), as D&D Beyond's Learn and Delete do. The left column keeps showing the state, with the green or struck-through bar for a change.

One Make for all, or each press sent at once?

My pick is one Make. A swap is two presses (one out, one in), and sending each at once would show the sheet half-swapped on D&D Beyond in between. One Make also gives one Undo.
