Changing spells and forms

You asked for Swap a form to work like Prepare spells, and for cantrips to sit in Prepare spells beside the other spells, as on D&D Beyond. The catch is that each list changes on its own rule, and a character with two spellcasting classes has two sets of rules. So every section of the window carries its own rule line, and the window asks why you're changing things, so it can mark which rules apply right now. Every board below works: tick, untick and switch the reason.

Sheet › Spells › Prepare spellsThe same large modal as today, now with cantrips (and a Wizard's spellbook) in it. The Swap a cantrip and Learn spells buttons go.
The Long Rest windowIts Prepare spells and Known forms buttons open these with the reason set to "After a Long Rest".
Battle's level-up card"You're Sorcerer 3" gets a Change spells button, opening with "I gained a Sorcerer level".
Sheet › Wild Shape › Known formsReplaces Swap a form's small window with the same large modal.

Two spellcasting classesEach class keeps its own rule, its own list and its own number, by its level in that class; "on a Sorcerer level" means a level in Sorcerer, not any level. Only spell slots are shared, worked out from the levels together. Filling new places when a class's number grows isn't a swap.

D&D Beyond enforces none of these; it shows Learn and Delete at any time. askdnd won't block either. It says each rule where you make the change, marks what applies now, and flags a change past it. The DM rules.

Your picks

Four things to decide

Two spellcasting classes: A (a tab per class) or B (one list)?

My pick is A: each tab shows only that class's rules, so the wrong rule is never beside the spell, and Your changes still shows both. B is closer to D&D Beyond but puts the two rule lines one above the other.

"Why are you changing?" at the top: keep it?

My pick is yes. It's what lets the window mark which rules apply now; it's set from where you opened it, and the player can change it. Without it, each rule line is shown and never marked Now or Not now.

A change past the rule: an amber note, and Make still works?

My pick is yes, as going over the prepared number works today (rules as written, the DM rules). The other way is a second press, "Make anyway", before it goes to D&D Beyond.

Known forms: the large modal for Wild Shape too?

As drawn in board 5, with pictures, grouped by CR, greyed Beasts kept with their reason. It replaces Swap a form's small window, from Sheet and the Long Rest window.
