Prepare for your turn

Your note (Nov 5): fights slow down when each player starts thinking only once their turn comes. While others take theirs, a player sketches their own: what they'll do with their Action, Bonus Action and movement, and the Reaction they're keeping. When their turn comes, each planned thing has its own Roll: roll what they want, in any order.

Where it livesYour turn's bar on askdnd's table (under the map), with the same plan on your Battle. It builds on Battle Combos, which already split a turn into these parts.
Who sees itOnly you, the player: it's a plan, not a promise. The DM sees only that you're ready (option D), never what you planned.
What it never doesPlay itself. Each roll is a press, the roll card as always, and anything can change when the fight does.

Option A

A scratch pad in your turn's bar

While others play, your turn's bar has Your scratch pad: a short list you jot from your Battle with + Add (an attack, a spell, a Bonus Action, a Reaction to keep). No slots to fill and nothing required (your note, Nov 13: start simple, then grow it from the table's feedback).

  • What each line costs is shown as you jot it (1 Focus Point, 2 left), so you don't plan what you can't afford.
  • It's cleared when your turn ends.

Option B

Your turn: the pad, each with Roll

When your turn begins, the pad is just there, each thing you jotted with its own Roll (or Cast) button, which opens the roll card as always. No order, nothing to skip, nothing to finish: roll what you want, in any order, and End turn when you're done (your note, Nov 13). What you rolled shows its result on its line.

  • Battle stays one click away for anything you didn't plan.
  • What a roll used is marked on your turn's bar as today (Action used).

Option D

The DM sees who's ready

A small dot on each portrait in the fight's band: ready (a plan made) or thinking (planning now). Only that, never the plan itself. Everyone at the table sees the dots, which gently nudges the next player.
