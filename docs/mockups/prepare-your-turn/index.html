Prepare for your turn

Your note (Nov 5): fights slow down when each player starts thinking only once their turn comes. While others take theirs, a player sketches their own: what they'll do with their Action, Bonus Action and movement, and the Reaction they're keeping. When their turn comes, they play it a step at a time, or do something else.

Where it livesYour turn's bar on askdnd's table (under the map), with the same plan on your Battle. It builds on Battle Combos, which already split a turn into these parts.
Who sees itOnly you, the player: it's a plan, not a promise. The DM sees only that you're ready (option D), never what you planned.
What it never doesPlay itself. Each step is a press, the roll card as always, and anything can change when the fight does.

Option A

A plan in your turn's bar

Your turn's bar has Your next turn while others play: four slots, Action, Bonus Action, Move and the Reaction you're keeping, each filled from your Battle with one press. A short note on Move says where you mean to go.

  • What a slot costs is shown as you plan (1 Focus Point, 2 left), so you don't plan what you can't afford.
  • It's kept until your turn ends, then cleared.

Option B

Your turn plays the plan, a step at a time

When your turn begins, the plan is the bar: the next step lit, with its button (Roll opens the roll card as always). A step done is ticked with what happened. Skip a step, or Do something else to put the plan aside and play from Battle.

  • The order is yours: drag a step up to do it first (Flurry before the attack, or after).
  • What a step used is ticked on your turn's bar as today (Action used, 15 ft moved).

Option D

The DM sees who's ready

A small dot on each portrait in the fight's band: ready (a plan made) or thinking (planning now). Only that, never the plan itself. Everyone at the table sees the dots, which gently nudges the next player.

Which ones?

My recommendation: A and B together (plan, then play it), with C's nudge. D is your call: it helps the pace, and some tables like it, others feel watched.
