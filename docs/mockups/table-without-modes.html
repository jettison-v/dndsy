Game Mode · designed again

No mode: your roll goes to the table

Game Mode is one switch doing four unrelated jobs, and it can be off in the middle of a fight. The bridge and the fight band now know enough that nobody should have to turn anything on. I recommend removing the switch.

What went wrong in your test

You were in a D&D Beyond encounter and the banner said Your turn, but Game Mode was off for the table. So askdnd rolled only in your browser: nothing reached Table rolls or the Game Log. The small "only in this browser" text was the only clue.

The pill didn't help: it said In step with D&D Beyond, which is about your sheet and says nothing about rolls.

Where you'll find the change

  1. Live window: the Game Mode switch and its timer are gone.
  2. The pill, top right: answers one question, whether your sheet is connected.
  3. The roll window, on every roll: Who sees it, and whether it reaches the Game Log.
  4. Table rolls, the left sidebar: always there, and it opens by itself in a fight.

Questions for you

1 · The switch

A, recommended: remove it. Rolls go to the table unless you say otherwise. Nothing to forget, and it works for skill checks outside a fight too.

B: turn it on by itself in a fight. Keeps Game Mode, and fixes your test. But a skill check in the middle of a session, with no fight running, would still be private.

C: keep the switch and make it louder. Smallest change, and someone still has to remember it.

2 · Rolls between sessions

With no switch, a player trying out a roll on a Tuesday would show up in Table rolls.

A, recommended: accept it. Rolls are shared by default, and anyone practising picks Only me, which is remembered. Simple, and one rule all the time.

B: start each roll on Only me unless someone else at the table is online. Fewer stray rolls, but who sees your roll changes without you doing anything, which is the confusion we're removing.

3 · Table rolls in a fight

A, recommended: it opens by itself when a fight starts and stays how you left it after that.

B: never opens by itself. Your choice alone, and easier to miss someone's roll.

4 · A roll that can't reach the Game Log

A, recommended: one quiet line in the roll window, as in the Sheet closed example, and the roll still goes to Table rolls.

B: stop and ask you to open your sheet first. The Game Log never misses a roll, but it slows every roll in a fight.
