Rolls · when it matters

Make it dramatic

Your idea (Nov 12): for the roll everyone is holding their breath for, the dice slow down. The whole table watches the same throw, big, in slow motion, and the number shows only once the die stops. It's a choice for the moment, never the default, so ordinary rolls stay quick.

Where it livesThe roll card (a press before Roll), every screen at the table (the throw), and Table rolls (the roll marked as dramatic).
Who uses itThe player rolling, for their own roll. The DM can also call it for someone's next roll, and use it for their own creatures' rolls.
Can digital dice do this?Yes, within limits. askdnd already chooses the result and the 3D dice are made to land on it, so the throw can be replayed slower, bigger and centred, with the number held back until it lands. It can't be truly unknown to the server, but nobody at the table sees the number before the die stops.

Option A

A press on the roll card

"Make it dramatic" sits with the roll's other choices. Pressed, the roll goes to the table as usual, but on every screen the dice are thrown big and slow in the middle, the roller's name and what's at stake above them, and the number shows only once the die lands (about four seconds, against about one now).

  • Only for the table's rolls: a DM-only or private roll stays quiet.
  • Works with the dice tray off or on: a dramatic roll always takes the middle of the screen, even for people who use the tray.
  • A natural 20 or 1 gets its own beat: a flash, and a sound for those who have dice sounds on.

Option B

Several rolls, one at a time

When a dramatic roll has several dice that each matter (a second beam, Extra Attack, Magic Missile's darts, a Flurry of Blows), they're thrown one after another, not all at once. Each result stays along the bottom, so the tension builds to the last one.

  • Damage can be dramatic too, for the hit that might drop the boss: the damage dice tumble last, the total held.
  • A line under the roller's name says what's at stake, when the DM has given one (option C), else just the roll's name.
  • Anyone can skip it on their own screen (Esc or a click); it plays on for everyone else.

Option C

The DM calls it

The DM knows when a roll matters before the player does. From the turn bar (or Party in a fight), the DM marks someone's next roll as dramatic, with an optional line about what's at stake. The player's card opens with "Make it dramatic" already pressed and "The DM called this one".

  • The DM's own rolls for creatures can be dramatic the same way (a dragon's breath, a saving throw against the party's big spell).
  • The line about what's at stake is the DM's to give away; askdnd never says a creature's Hit Points on its own.

Option D

Automatic for death saves, and the table's limits

Some rolls always matter: a death save. The campaign can make every death save dramatic, set how slow, and how often a player may make a roll dramatic, so it stays special (once a turn, once a fight, or no limit).
