One door, and its name: my view

  • One door, yes. Only an admin sees both today, so only you would notice the change: one door with two halves, This campaign (the DM's week: Needs you, Inbox, Sessions…) and askdnd (For you first, then running askdnd). A DM sees only the first half, a player sees no door. The gear was named for settings, but For you, Feedback and Testing are your daily work with Claude, not settings; as one door its count rides on the door, and the odd-sized gear leaves the bar.
  • Call it Backstage. Everything in it is what the table doesn't see while playing: the DM's preparation and review, and running askdnd. One word, plain, in the table's language. Workspace is the safe second (it's what we called the DM's page). Manage reads as account settings and fits the new tools least. Behind the screen is lovely for the DM but long, and wrong for the admin half.
  • Manage › Settings stays, as this campaign's settings, inside the door's first half: with the gear gone, “Settings” means one thing.
  • Under your picture: no mark. You're right that it's uncommon. On the web an avatar is you, not a place: GitHub, Google, Slack and Notion never mark it when you're on your profile or settings; the page's own title says where you are, and the open menu ticks the page. The one common exception is a phone's bottom tab bar (Instagram rings your picture when your profile tab is open), where the picture is a tab. In a top bar a line under a circle reads like a status badge. So: one mark for places and menus, none for you.
