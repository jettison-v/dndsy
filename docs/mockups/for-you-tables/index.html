Why I'd pick three, quick ones first

Three tables: Decide and From the Roadmap are both quick to open, but they're different work. A Decide row is done the moment you press, and it often unblocks something of mine. A Roadmap row is the Roadmap's own: a scope you read with care, or a step of yours that can be an evening's job (today's are "Audit the roll card" and "Take a second look at the table"). They also sort differently: Decide by area, the Roadmap by your order of importance. In one table, a two-minute answer and an evening's step look alike.

Quick ones first: answers take seconds, and a waiting answer is often what's holding my work, so they're worth clearing before a round that takes ten minutes or more. Try is the biggest table, so at the bottom it has room to grow, and starting a round takes you off this page anyway. If you'd rather start with what takes longest, pick Try first; that's how it's laid out today.

Decided, unless you say otherwise
  • An empty table stays as its heading plus one line ("Nothing to decide"), without column headings, so the page keeps its shape and you can see it's empty. Pick Almost nothing to see it.
  • Try's heading holds its round: ▶ Start round for the device you're on, with the count and minutes. Checks for another device or for more than one person get a group row of their own, saying where to do them.
  • Each table has its own columns. Try: Kind, What and its goal, Takes. Decide: Kind, What, From. From the Roadmap: Kind, Item, What's yours.
  • The summary line, Backstage's count and the order inside each table stay as they are today.
