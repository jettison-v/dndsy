How the work flows: askdnd runs it, Claude works it

You asked for the architecture first, a picture of it, the chat setup, and real pushback. Your idea was expert chats per Product Area, a listener chat that hands work out, and me as architect and reviewer. This page is my answer, with picks at the bottom.

In short

Yes to askdnd doing the sequencing and the status, with clear rules. No to more standing chats. The distributor has to be askdnd's own code, not a chat, because a chat can't listen between turns and is one more thing that can go quiet unseen. Expertise belongs in the Map and the tests, not in a chat's memory. So: askdnd is the dispatcher, sessions are short-lived workers that hold a claim on one task, and one lead session (the one you talk to) is architect and reviewer. It's built so channels plug in when they leave preview, with nothing else changing.

What makes something Ready, and how it's picked up

Today

At the start of a session and before each round of work, I ask askdnd for the next thing (feedback.py next). It names the top of your order and lists what it skipped because it waits on you. But that's me following a rule: nothing stops me working on something else, and nothing records that I took it. Today's misses lived in that gap.

Proposed

Ready is a state askdnd works out from facts, never my judgment, and a session doesn't choose its work: it asks, and askdnd hands it over and records the claim.

Ready means all four are true

1It has your go-ahead

An issue you filed, or a player's note you approved (your OK for its fix); for a Roadmap item, your Scope looks right.

2Nothing it waits on is open

No question to you on it, no step of yours, nothing it depends on unfinished.

3It isn't in your way

Its area isn't in a round you have open. Paused (game night) holds merging, not building, so it can still be Ready.

4Nobody holds it

No session has a live claim on it.

Anything that isn't Ready says why where you see it: waiting for your OK, your round, or your answer.

How it's picked up

  1. A session asks askdnd for work. The lead, when it finishes something or when its listener hears that something became Ready.
  2. askdnd hands over the top Ready piece in your one order: anything broken at the table first, then Roadmap items from the top, fixes grouped by Product Area. It records who has it.
  3. The session checks in every few minutes while it works. A claim that goes quiet for 20 minutes goes back to Ready, and the bar shows whose it was.
  4. The claim ends when its pull request is attached. From there askdnd moves it on by itself: In review, Live, yours to try.

Your questions

How do workers get their context, and what do they cost?

askdnd can't start a Claude session; it's a website. The lead starts a worker and hands it the brief askdnd writes: the issue in your words, its Product Area's part of the Map, the check to pass, the files involved. The worker never sees this chat.

A small fix by a worker costs about what the lead would spend on it, plus 10–20% for reading in. Workers don't save money; they keep the lead free to talk with you and to review. Two at once costs twice as much, so start with one.

Does Claude still have to listen?

Yes, for now: something has to notice what you send. The difference: listening becomes a claim in askdnd that checks in, so when it stops the bar says so at once, and the Mini covers the time when no session is open.

Once channels leave preview, askdnd pushes into an open session and the listening loop goes away; a session still has to be open.

How is the Mini involved?

It's the always-on computer in your house that already runs the nightly test. A small script asks askdnd every minute or two: is anything waiting on Claude, and is anyone listening? If something has waited 10 minutes with nobody listening, it notifies your phone (step 3b).

Later it could start a session itself; that spends money with nobody watching, so it's your call after the notification has proven itself.

Lives in askdnd

Every decision, pick, OK, mark and note; the order of work; every state and who holds it; what each pull request is doing. If I said it in chat, I write it onto the item.

Lives in the repo

How each feature works (the Map), the rules (tests), decisions (the log), the pages you read. A fresh worker reads its area's part in a minute; nothing depends on a chat remembering.

Lives in a chat

Only the conversation with you, and the work in hand. A chat can be summarized or closed at any time without losing anything that matters.

The chat setup: three ways, argued

A · Today: one chat does everything

No handoffs, one memory, design stays with me.

Against: one turn at a time; while I build, notes wait; when the chat is summarized or idle, nobody listens. Today's misses mostly came from here.

B · Your idea: experts per area, a listener chat, me as architect

Parallel work, and someone always "on".

  • A listener chat can't really listen. A chat acts only during a turn, costs tokens while it waits, and is one more thing that can go quiet unseen: today's first miss, in two places.
  • Standing experts go stale. A long chat gets summarized (this one has been); the real expertise is the Map, tests and issues, which a fresh session reads quickly. The per-area trial on Nov 17 left gaps.
  • Ten Product Areas, ten chats to keep awake, and parallel writers collide: today a scheduled session's edits landed in my working copy.
  • The bottleneck is your time to try and decide, not my hands. More builders means more waiting in For you and more checks superseded before you reach them.
C · My pick: askdnd dispatches, a lead, short-lived workers

askdnd hands out work by its rules. I stay the one you talk to: architect, designer, reviewer and the one who merges. A worker starts from askdnd's queue with a brief askdnd writes (the issue, its Map section, its checks), in its own copy of the code, holds a claim with a heartbeat, and ends at a pull request I review. The Mini notices when work waits and no session is open, and wakes one (or pings your phone).

Against my own pick: workers cost more tokens than one chat, and a worker designing screens would give you piecemeal UI. So workers take only clear fixes and behind-the-scenes work; anything you'll look at stays with the lead. Start with one worker, not two, and earn the second.

When channels leave preview

Channels push a message into a running session, so I hear you without waiting on a poll. In this design that only swaps the dashed delivery box: askdnd keeps an outbox per listener, today's wait reads it, a channel would push the same outbox, and the Mini covers the case where no session is open. The queue, states, gates and timers don't change.

Devil's advocate on channels themselves: they'd have fixed one of today's five misses (my listener). The stuck pull requests, the check left without a replacement and the unopenable page are bookkeeping, and no delivery fixes bookkeeping. That's why the order below starts with askdnd, not with channels.
