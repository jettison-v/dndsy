Coins in more than one place

Today the Coins window only spends and gains what a character carries. A bag's coins are only shown, with “change a bag's coins on D&D Beyond”. Your notes 52 and 53 ask for two things: choose which bag when spending or gaining, and move coins between bags. Below are three ways the window could work.

Where it lives

Sheet › Items (Inventory on Battle). The purse at the top of the list is one line, 7,654 GP68 SP92 CP, counting every bag. Pressing it opens the Coins window. Nothing else on the page changes.

What D&D Beyond allows

Every change names its placeD&D Beyond's own coin changes say where the coins are: the character, or one of its containers. So askdnd can spend from or add to a bag the same way it does for carried coins today.
No “move” of its ownD&D Beyond has no call that moves coins. A move is two changes made together: take them out of one place, put them into the other. If the second one doesn't go through, askdnd puts the first one back.
Any container can hold coinsA backpack, a pouch or a Bag of Holding. The window lists only the places that already hold coins, plus any other container you pick as the place to put them.
My pick

A. Spend · Gain · Move

Three tabs at the top of the window, each one a sentence you fill in. Carried is picked unless you choose a bag. With coins in only one place, nothing about bags appears: the window looks like it does today.

Good at
  • One clear verb at a time, the same on a phone and a computer.
  • Spending from carried coins (the everyday case) takes the same taps as today.
  • A move shows the result before it happens.
Costs
  • One change per press: splitting a haul into two bags takes two moves.

B. Pick a place, then act

The window opens as a list of places, each with its coins. Tap a place: its row opens with an amount, Spend, Gain and Move to…. No tabs; the place comes first.

Good at
  • You see every place's coins first, then act on one.
  • Fits D&D Beyond's own coin manager, which works per container.
Costs
  • Spending carried coins takes one tap more than today (pick Carried first).
  • Moving into a container that holds no coins yet is less obvious: it's under Move to…, not in the list.

C. A purse table

One table: a row per place, a column per coin. Type new numbers, see the changes listed, and press Make once. Moving is lowering one row and raising another.

  • Many changes at once: splitting loot, tidying after a shopping trip.

  • Typing a new total instead of “spend 25”: you do the sum yourself.
  • Cramped on a phone: five columns of numbers in a small window.
  • A move isn't a move to askdnd, just two edits, so Undo puts back each separately.

The same choice everywhere coins move

Whichever you pick, the other places that change coins follow it, so “which bag” means the same thing everywhere:

Buying in the item browserPaying from the purse asks Pay from (Carried, or a bag) when coins are in more than one place. Today it only pays from carried coins.
The DM's Give lootCoins land in Carried, as they do now. The player moves them with Move.
Spending a coin you don't haveSpending 5 gp with only silver could make change, the way buying already does (largest coins first, change given back). Today the window refuses. Say if you want this too.
My recommendation: A (Spend · Gain · Move).

Spending carried coins is by far the most common case at the table, and A keeps it exactly as quick as today. A bag only shows up when it holds coins. Move is its own tab, so it reads plainly, and it shows the result before you press it. C is better for big tidy-ups but slower for the everyday spend, and hard to use on a phone.
