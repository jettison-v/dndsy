Why areas

  • They're how a fix is tried: the Item Builder's three notes are fixed together, looked at together and come back as one run of checks, never spread through a round.
  • They're how I keep quality: one area's pages and code in my head at a time, finished (fixed, looked at on a laptop and a phone, reviewed, merged) before the next.
  • They're the record a fresh session starts from (Board 7): the notes, my questions, your answers, the checks.

The five steps

Found (sorted into the area) · Questions (anything I need from you first, or nothing) · Claude fixing (one area only) · Ready for you (its checks are live and in For you) · Done (every check passed).

A typed roll that isn't dice

You asked

What happens in play when a typed roll isn't a dice roll, and why have a free-form box at all?

How I ask

How they stay that way

The rules go into docs/testing-guide.md, which I follow for every check, and a test reads every check I add: a step with two actions in it (“, then”), a typed value not marked as typed, or a check that makes something without saying where it runs is refused before it reaches you.

Fresh sessions: a trial, with a way back

The trial

Each area gets a fresh session, which I start for you when its turn comes; it shows in your sidebar beside this one. It starts from the area's record in askdnd (its notes, my questions and your answers, its checks), the decision log, and the area's parts of docs/MAP.md.

Its first message tells you what it read and what it's about to do, so a gap shows before any code changes. A long chat gets summarized as it grows, and each summary drops detail; a fresh session per area avoids that.

The way back

Last time fresh sessions left gaps, so the trial ends at the first sign of one: a session that asks what you already answered, misses a decision, or breaks something that was settled. Then we go back to one chat, this one, and the areas run here one after another, the same way.

You can call it any time: say “back to one chat”. Either way, the record lives in askdnd, not in a chat.

Building it: about M in all, in three slices, each tried by you before the next. 1: For you by area, the walkthrough (rules, marks, ticks), several issues per check, the card's corners. 2: areas and Where it stands, Go into VTT Test. 3: questions and picks in askdnd. The Testing card's own notes (83, 85, 91, 95) are fixed in slice 1, since you'll test everything after it with that card.
