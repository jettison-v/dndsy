Telling your notes apart

Today every note on Your notes looks the same: a status pill, your words in a big quote, then the answer underneath in small grey text, all at full width. With thirty notes it's hard to see which is which, what was done about it, and where it stands. Below are three layouts. They share one idea: every note has the same three parts, in both tabs.

The three parts, in both tabs

1 · You askedA short title: the first sentence of what you wrote, cut to one line, so a list of notes is a list of different things. Your whole note, and the page you were on, are one tap away.
2 · What happenedAbout askdnd: Claude's words on how it was fixed and where to see it, or why not. About the story: where your words ended up (added to an entry, or worked into a recap), with a link. A green box once it's done, grey while it's still open.
3 · Where it standsA track of dots with the steps for that tab, the current one lit:
SentApprovedPlannedFixed
SentWith the DMAdded or used
One that isn't taken up stops where it was turned down, in grey, and the steps it skipped are struck through:
SentClosed by an adminPlannedFixed
SentApprovedClosed by ClaudeFixed
SentNot used by the DMAdded or used

The ones you turn down (your comment): a note you delete on the feedback queue, one Claude closes, and a story note the DM marks Not used all go to Done with the grey track above, and What happened says why. Today a note you delete tells its sender only “Not something askdnd will change for now”, so Delete on the queue would ask for a reason first (one line, for the sender, which you can leave blank), shown in What happened. The DM's Not used works the same way if you pick “let the DM reply” below. A note turned down stays on the sender's page with its reason. As today, it can't be added to; they can send a new one.

The two tabs stay as they are (About the story, the DM's; About askdnd, Brett's then Claude's), each with its count of what's new. Inside both, the notes are split into Open and Done, open first, so what you're still waiting on is at the top.

A. A card per note

Each note is one card: the title and the track across the top, then You asked and What happened side by side (stacked on a phone). Open notes first, then Done. Everything is visible without a click.

What happened

Add to it: something you forgot, or what happened next

Search and filters (your comment), the list pattern's own, as on Sheet › Items and the item browser: search looks through your words and the answers; a chip per status with its count, and New to you; Sent from picks a page (Battle, Sheet, Story…); the order is newest or oldest first. Open and Done stay the list's two headings whatever is filtered. On a phone, search and the chips sit above the list, the chips in one row that scrolls.

About the story is the same layout: the dot is violet once the DM used it, the title is the first line of your suggestion, and What happened says where it went (“Added to Pip's entry”, “Worked into the recap of Session 18”) with a link to it. Its chips are Waiting, Added, Used and Not used, and Sent from becomes About (an entry, a session's recap, the glossary).

Good at
  • Thirty notes fit on one screen, each a different title.
  • Open and Done are separate at a glance; the dot's colour says where each stands.
  • The same layout as the quest log and the World tab, so it's already familiar.
Costs
  • One click to read an answer in full (its first words are on the row).
  • Search and filters take a row above the list (worth it past a dozen notes).

C. A conversation per note

Each note is one line until you open it, then reads like a chat: your words on the right, each step in the middle (“An admin approved it · Nov 7”), and the answer on the left, green once it's fixed. Adding to it is replying.

My recommendation: B, a list with the note beside it.

Your three goals map onto it directly: the title tells notes apart, the dot and the first words of the answer on each row say where it stands and what happened, and the note on the right gives all three parts in full. It is also askdnd's existing list pattern (the quest log), so it's built from the design system rather than a new kind of page, and it holds up at thirty notes and more. The track of dots goes on the open note in either tab.
