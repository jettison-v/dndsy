Manage › Table · decided and built, Oct 30

One list of who's at the table

Today a person and their character are in two separate lists, and removing someone is an option hidden in a dropdown. This puts each person and their character on one row, with one Add button, and says exactly what Remove will do before it does it.

Why it's confusing today

  • Two lists for one idea. Characters says who plays them. People says what they play, but you can't change it there.
  • Removing is hidden. You take someone out by picking No role in a dropdown and pressing Save.
  • A character can't be removed at all. It can only be moved to another campaign, on another page.
  • Adding takes two forms. Add the person, then add the character, then connect them.
  • Three pages. Table, Accounts and Campaigns each hold part of the job.

Where you'll find it

  1. Open Manage, then Table, in the campaign you're changing.
  2. One list: the DM first, then each player with their character.
  3. + Add to the table at the top does a person, a character, or both.
  4. Click any row for everything about it, including Remove.
  5. Accounts stays for who can sign in to askdnd at all. Most of the time you won't need it.

Try it

Click a row, try Remove, or add someone. Pretend page, and the Reset button puts everything back.

Questions for you

1 · One list or two?

A, recommended: one list, as above. A person and their character read as one seat at the table, which is how you think about it.

B: keep two lists and add Remove buttons to each. Smaller change, but the two lists still have to be read together.

2 · Who can change the table?

Today only admins can; the DM can only look.

A, recommended: the DM runs their own table. They add people who already have an account, change roles and characters, and remove. A brand-new email, which lets someone sign in to askdnd, stays admin only.

B: admins only, as today. Simpler, but your DM has to ask you each time. It matters most when another group joins.

3 · What does Remove do?

A, recommended: nothing is ever deleted. They move to a folded Left the table list with Bring back, so a mistake costs nothing. Their story, rolls and notes stay.

B: test copies delete for good, and everything else stays. A cleaner list, but it can't be undone.

4 · Moving a character to another campaign

A, recommended: Move to, in the character's row. Everything about the character is in one place, and Admin › Campaigns stops being a place you have to remember.

B: leave it on Admin › Campaigns. It's rare, and that page shows every campaign side by side.
