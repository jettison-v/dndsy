Building an item a piece at a time, then giving it

Your note from DM Tools (feedback 72): making an item is hard because every option shows at once. Build it piece by piece instead (add a roll, add charges), with more pickers and less typing, and look first at how other tools do it. This is also the Roadmap's step 2: Give to a character, which puts the item on their D&D Beyond sheet.

Board 1 · the item's page

Only what the item has, and + Add for the rest

A new item starts with its core (name, kind, rarity, attunement, its words) and nothing under What it does in play. + Add opens a short menu of what askdnd can play; picking one adds that piece, opened, ready to fill in. Close it and it folds to its one line.

Board 2 · a piece, opened

Pickers that write a sentence

Opening a piece shows only its own questions, each a row of chips or a − + stepper: how it lands, the dice, what it does, the save and DC, what a success does, and what it costs from the item's charges. The sentence at the top updates as you pick, so you read what you made instead of checking boxes.

Words stay typed only where they're words: the item's name and what the table reads. The dice box still takes typing for anyone faster that way; it fills the pickers.

Board 3 · + New item

Start from something

The advice in every tool's forum is the same: start from something close and change it. + New item asks once what it starts from:

  • Nothing: today's blank item.
  • A weapon or armor from the 2024 Player's Handbook: its dice, properties, weight and cost come with it, so a magic weapon is the base plus its bonuses. A bonus added to it is "with this item" already (askdnd's Only with this item, ticked for you).
  • A copy of an item: one of the campaign's own, or a book's magic item askdnd plays (its charges, rolls and spells come as pieces you can change). The copy is the campaign's own; the book's stays as it is.

Board 4 · Give to a character (the Roadmap's step 2)

The DM gives it; the player takes it onto their sheet

On D&D Beyond only a sheet's own player can change it, so giving works the way giving healing does today: the DM picks who, and that player gets a card on every askdnd page they have open, with Take it. Their bridge adds the item to their D&D Beyond inventory as a custom item (its name, its words, cost and weight: all D&D Beyond keeps for one), and askdnd plays what it does, because it's carried by name. Undo takes it back off.
