The roll workflow, four ways

Before the action roll becomes a component (see the Component Scan), a choice of how it should feel. Every option is drawn for the same moment, the one today's flow handles worst: No Name attacks Enemy 1 with an Unarmed Strike while concentrating on Hunter's Mark, with Stunning Strike to spend if it hits. The numbers are examples.

Where it lives

Every option starts the same way: the +7 on an attack's row on Battle (or the same row on Sheet, a spell's Cast, a save in the side panel). On a computer the roll opens beside the row; on a phone it rises from the bottom, within thumb reach. What the table sees lands in Table rolls as it does today, and the dice are thrown on everyone's screen. The options differ in what happens between pressing +7 and the damage reaching Enemy 1.

A. Roll at once, decide after

3 presses

Pressing +7 rolls straight away with what askdnd knows (Advantage if a rule gives it, said on the result). Everything else is decided on the result: Advantage after the fact, damage with the riders ticked in, then the target. The window before the roll is gone; a long press (or ⋯) still opens it for the rare roll that needs it.

B. One attack card

2–3 presses

Pressing +7 opens a card that shows the whole attack: to hit and damage side by side, Advantage and why at the top, what adds to the damage already ticked when it applies (Hunter's Mark while you concentrate). One Roll throws both. The result stays on the same card: if it hits, the after-hit choices (Stunning Strike, Divine Smite, Sneak Attack once a turn) are one press each, and Apply to Enemy 1 finishes it.

Good at
  • An attack is one card from start to end: nothing to remember, nothing to add up.
  • Riders show where they apply: before the roll if they always add (Hunter's Mark), after a hit if you choose them then (Stunning Strike, Smite).
  • Checks and saves stay the short version: one press, mode on the card.
Costs
  • Damage is rolled even on a miss (Missed sets it aside; the table sees it struck through).
  • A bigger card than today's window, so on a phone it takes the lower half of the screen.

C. A guided sequence

5 presses

The same window walks through the action one step at a time, showing only the steps this action has: Before (Advantage and why) → To hit → Hit or miss (you say what the DM said) → Damage (riders) → Apply. A bar at the top shows where you are; Back goes a step back. A save spell's steps would be Before → DC → Damage → Apply.

D. A roll dock

3 presses

A dock at the bottom of Battle (the edge on a computer) holds the next roll, the way a game's action bar does. Pressing +7 loads the attack into the dock; its chips are what can change it (Advantage, Hunter's Mark, Bless's d4 from someone else). One big Roll; results stack above it, newest on top, each with its next step. The dock stays: the next roll loads in the same place.

My recommendation: B, one attack card.

It turns an attack into one thing from start to finish, puts each rider where the rules put it (Hunter's Mark adds to every hit, so it's on before the roll; Stunning Strike and Divine Smite are chosen after a hit, so they're on the result), and keeps checks and saves as quick as they are now. It's also the shape the component needs anyway: a card drawn from the action's roll plan, so a spell attack, a save spell, a weapon with Sneak Attack and a creature's attack are all the same card with different steps. A's "roll at once" could be a setting on top later for experienced players.
