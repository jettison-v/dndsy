Filters above a list

Where a list sits under its filters in a narrow column, the filters fold to one control. The first build drew it as a bold FILTERS heading with what's on squeezed beside it, which clouded what's filtered, and with three kinds on it overflowed World's column. This round draws two designs carefully: at askdnd's real sizes, in each place the folded filters go, at that place's real width, and in every state, including three on and many long names.

A · A control like Sort, with what's on as pillsMy pick

The control says Filters; the pills say what's filtered

The control is drawn like Sort's dropdown (the same height, border and regular weight), so the two read as a pair. What's on sits beside it as blue pills, each with its own ×; at most two show, then "+2", so it never takes more than two lines. Clear comes at the end. Open, the pills give way to the chips themselves.

C · A bar that says what's showing

One line, in words

One field-like bar: "Showing every note", or "Showing Something's broken · Planned" in regular text, Clear, and the arrow. It's always one line, so the list never moves down, but many filters cut off with "…" (the whole list is its tooltip), and taking one filter off means opening it.

Side by side

What each is better at

A, pills
  • What's on is unmistakable: the same blue as the chips you pressed.
  • Take one filter off without opening (its ×).
  • Matches Sort's dropdown beneath it.
  • Two lines when several are on.
C, a bar
  • Always one line: the list never moves.
  • Reads as a sentence, like the search box above it.
  • Many filters cut off ("…").
  • Taking one off means opening it.

How should the folded filters look?
  • A, a control like Sort with what's on as pills (my pick): clearest about what's filtered, one × each, two lines at most.
  • C, a "Showing …" bar: one line always, in words.

Whichever you pick, the build adds layout tests for these same states in these same places, so a long filter line can't push past its column again.
