2 · What the established tools do

They all keep the same five things apart

Test management (TestRail, Xray and Zephyr for Jira, Qase, Testmo), issue trackers (Linear, Jira, GitHub) and feedback tools (Marker.io, BugHerd, Usersnap, Jam, Sentry's User Feedback; Canny and Productboard for ideas), read from their own documentation.

The thingWhat it isTheir namesWhat they agree on
A reportWhat someone sent, with its evidence (screenshot, marks, page, browser)Feedback (Sentry, Marker.io), Note (Productboard), a jam (Jam)One capture tool for anyone; guests get a short form and see only their own; it becomes an issue in triage
TriageDeciding what a report becomesTriage inbox (Linear), Feedback column (BugHerd)Accept, decline with a reason, merge into a duplicate, or wait for an answer
An issueThe work: what gets fixedIssue (Linear, GitHub), Defect (Qase, Xray), BugClosed with a reason (fixed, not planned, duplicate); a merged change can mark it fixed by itself (Linear, GitHub)
A checkWhat to try, written once and tried many timesTest case (all of them)Steps and an expected result; kept apart from any one try of it
A resultOne try of a check: who, when, how it wentResult (TestRail, Qase, Testmo), Test run (Xray), Execution (Zephyr)Never edited: a retest adds a new result and the old failure stays in the history; the latest result is where it stands

Where they agree, in short

  • Failing is one action: the status, your words, the picture and "a new issue, or one already filed", in one window, as many issues as you found (TestRail, Xray, Qase).
  • Failed is set by the tester, never worked out from the issues.
  • Results are append-only. A mistake is corrected by a new result (TestRail allows editing only for a short while, with its own permission; Qase locks finished runs).
  • A fix sends the check back to whoever failed it to try again; trying it again stays a person's step (BugHerd: "Done, not yet verified").
  • "Needs info" is a waiting state that flips back when the reporter answers (Jira Service Management).
  • Ideas have their own intake from bugs (Canny, Productboard: votes, merging, "tell me when it ships").

Where they differ, and what I'd take

  • Retest: a status (TestRail, Testmo) or a new run (Xray, Zephyr, Qase). Take: a new result, shown as Try again.
  • Moving forward by itself: only trackers do it (Linear's pull request rules). Take: askdnd already does it (a fix goes live, its checks come back), so keep it.
  • Can't try it: "Blocked" in every test tool, with why. Take: it replaces Skip.
  • What reporters see: their own reports and the replies meant for them, never the internal notes (Marker.io, BugHerd). askdnd already works this way for players.

Sources: TestRail, Xray, Zephyr, Qase, Testmo, Linear, Jira, GitHub, Marker.io, BugHerd, Usersnap, Jam, Sentry, Canny and Productboard help pages (the research report is kept with this page).

4 · The workflows

Who does what, end to end

You · a player · Claude · askdnd by itself

Someone finds something

1

Anyone presses the feedback button on any page: words, the picture (marked up, or one they add), what it is (a problem, an idea, a question).

2

You approve a player's report; yours go straight on. Approving files it as an issue, or as more evidence on one already filed ("same as").

3

Claude sorts the issue into an area and answers its sender.

Claude fixes an area

1

Claude takes the first area in your order, reads all of it together, and asks you what only you can decide (the issue says Claude asks; you answer in For you).

2

Claude fixes it as one change and writes a check for each issue (or adds the issue to a check it already has).

3

askdnd: once the change is live, its issues turn Fixed and their checks come back as Try again, to whoever failed them (a player's to that player first).

You try a check

1

You start the area's round; each check opens its page with the walkthrough card.

2

It works: ✓ Passed. Its issues close as verified.

3

It doesn't: ✗ Something's wrong…, your words and the picture. The first one marks this try Failed; each problem you find after it is the same button again, added to the same try. No separate + Note while trying, nothing to fail at the end, nothing written twice.

4

You can't try it (the data isn't there, the check is written wrong): Can't try… with why. Claude fixes the check.

5

Pressed the wrong one: Change result on the latest result (see pick 4).

Something that works, but…

1

On a check that works with something to add: ✓ Passed, then + Note files an idea (or a small problem) from it without failing it.

2

Anywhere else: the feedback button, as always.

So a check has three marks, each one action: Passed, Something's wrong…, Can't try…; + Note is the only extra.

6 · Where the Roadmap fits

A plan above it all: the macro view

You log enhancements with the feedback button today, and the Roadmap is coming into askdnd, not yet designed. This is only how they'd fit together, so this model doesn't paint the Roadmap into a corner; the Roadmap gets its own mockup. The tools have the same layer: Linear's projects and initiatives, Jira's epics, Productboard's features.

What the Roadmap adds

One object, the roadmap item: its title, why, whose turn (yours or Claude's, with exactly what to do), its order, its size, and links to its plan and mockups. Kept as data in askdnd, not written by hand on a page.

What it reads from this model

Its areas' stages, so an item says "Claude fixing", "Ready for you" or "Done" by itself; the picks waiting on you; the pull requests that shipped it. Your "Yours" steps and For you become the same list.

What it decides here

Which area comes next: the Roadmap's order, the areas under the item at the top. Problems found while trying land in that item's area; a new idea waits to be accepted onto an item.

7 · Built to lift out

What makes it ready for another project, or a company

Its own pieces

Its own tables (reports, issues, checks, results, areas, events) and pages, talking to the host app through one small adapter: who the people are and their roles, how to view as one of them, what the pages are, where pictures are stored. askdnd becomes its first host.

Roles

Reporter (sends, sees their own), Tester (tries checks), Triager (approves, merges, closes), Developer (a person or Claude: fixes, writes checks, asks), Admin. A company maps its own groups onto these.

A full history

Every change to any of them is an event: who, what, when, from what to what. Never edited or deleted; states can be retired but never removed, so old history keeps its meaning (Qase's rule).

Connections

Issues sync both ways with Jira, Linear or GitHub through a map of states (Qase, Marker.io do this); a merged change marks its issues fixed (Linear, GitHub "fixes #n"); notices by email or Slack.

The developer can be Claude

What's new since Claude last read is its inbox (what feedback.py's inbox and wait read today), its questions are answered in For you, and its key is its own: the part no tool in the research has, and the reason to build rather than buy.

What stays askdnd's

The table's own rules: a player's report waits for you, the DM never sees a player's character notes in a picture, first names only to Claude.

8 · Your picks

Six decisions

You'll answer them in Testing's For you, mine marked. Then I write the plan in slices; the first slice changes nothing you'd notice except the fixes for notes 111 to 116 and 121.

1 · Report and issue: one thing or two?

Today a note is both what was sent and the work.

A · Two, shown as one MineA report is what someone sent; an issue is the work. Usually one each, but several reports of the same thing join one issue (each sender still hears back). Feedback shows the issue with its reports inside.
B · OneA note stays both; a duplicate is closed with "same as" a link. Simpler now, harder for a company with many reporters.

2 · Failing a check

Notes 115 and 116.

A · One button, as you go Mine✗ Something's wrong… sends a problem and marks the try Failed; each further problem is the same button, joining the same try. + Note only after Passed, for "it works, but…".
B · As now, simplifiedFailed… fails it with the first issue; + Another issue adds more; + Note sends one without failing, and Failed after those asks "fail with these?"

3 · Can't try a check

Skip today, always with a reason.

A · Can't try… MineThe test tools' "Blocked": you couldn't try it (why), so it comes back to Claude, not to Done. Skip goes away.
B · Keep SkipSkipped with a reason, and it counts as done.

4 · Pressing the wrong mark

Tonight you picked "nothing you'd see" for Undo Failed, and it's built. Every test tool does the opposite: the record stays, and a correction is added.

A · Change result, kept in the history MineThe latest result can be changed; the history shows "Failed, changed to Try again by you", and any issue it filed is closed as withdrawn (still findable), not deleted. The record of what happened stays true, which a company will need.
B · Nothing you'd see (tonight's)Undo takes the Failed and its issues out of the history as if it never happened.
