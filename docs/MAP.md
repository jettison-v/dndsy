# Where things are

The map of askdnd's features: where each lives, the rules it keeps and the decisions behind it. Moved out of CLAUDE.md on Nov 13 (Brett: development faster; CLAUDE.md is read on every turn, this only when needed). Read a feature's section before changing it, and keep it current in the same change.

Ordered by the Product Taxonomy (app/data/taxonomy.json; Brett, Nov 18): a heading per Product Area, its Features' sections under it in the list's order. A session taking up a Product Area reads its heading's sections. A new section goes under its Feature's area and is named in that Feature's `map` (tests/test_taxonomy.py keeps the two the same).

## Battle

- **Hit points window** (app/hit_points.py, `docs/mockups/hp-window.html`; step 5.4, 2026-10-28): the DM's rolls for a monster reach Table rolls from D&D Beyond's game log, heard on any player's open sheet (`ddb_bridge.heard_in_game`, kept once by roll id; D&D Beyond's roll has no damage type, so `hit_points.attack_type` takes it from the monster's attack in the rules data). Damage the DM rolled and healing anyone rolled offer **Apply to** (the player for their own character, the DM for anyone), which opens the window (`_hp_window.html`, a modal); − and + on Battle's Hit points tile open the same form in the small window, ready to type in, Enter takes it (`_hp_quick.html`, `_hp_form.html`; Brett 2026-11-01, `docs/mockups/hp-flow/` Option B), and Hit points' panel still offers it on the pages without them. `hit_points.work` is the 2024 rules as one tested function (defenses with their sources, a form's too, crossable for a ruling; Temporary Hit Points first; Concentration's DC; at 0 and massive damage). Applying makes an `hp` or `temp` bridge change and a follow-up row of the roll (kind "applied", carrying the Concentration save the player rolls from their Table rolls). An applied row moves its roll up Table rolls but throws nobody's dice again: a row's `data-thrown` counts only the follow-ups that add dice (damage, reroll, bonus), and only those are thrown on other screens (Brett's test, Nov 16). The Hit Points beside each name in Apply to… follow the character, not the roll: each pill is marked `data-hp-of`, and a live change to that character fetches `/hp/now` (only the characters this person may apply to) and brings it up to date; tests/browser/test_apply_to.py.
- **Conditions on Battle** (app/conditions.py `edge`/`shown`, `_cond_icons.html`, `docs/mockups/conditions-on-battle.html`; step 5, 2026-10-28): each condition has its own colour (app.css `--cond-*`, by `[data-cond]` slug) and an icon drawn for askdnd. Concentration is first in the Status row (`_conc_pill.html`) and listed like a condition (`character_pages.listed` puts it first; always on the edge). Battle draws the page's edge in the newest three's colours (Blinded or Unconscious also darken it); every character page has a badge per condition after the name (`cond_badges`, character.html; Brett 2026-10-29, `docs/mockups/temporary-items.html`); a condition that starts pops its badge in and brightens the edge once (app.js `condArrive`). Party cards the same badges after each name, your character's menu bar button a dot each (`live.rows_from`; swapped with the page like the Live pill). **Synced with D&D Beyond**: the 15 conditions and Exhaustion are read from the sheet (`cond.from_sheet`, summary `conditions`); always show what a character is under through `character_pages.listed` / `cond.shown`, never `Character.conditions` alone (it holds askdnd's own: item effects, and where a condition came from). Where the bridge works, starting or ending one is a `set condition:<id>` or `exhaustion` change.
- **Death saves** (app/death_saves.py, `docs/mockups/death-saves.html`; step 6, 2026-10-29): at 0 Hit Points the HP tile is the Dying tile (`_char.html` `hp_tile`; the marks are the sheet's `death_saves`). A death save rolled while dying marks itself through the bridge (`table_rolls.death_mark` for table rolls, `POST /characters/<id>/death` for private ones; a reroll moves the mark); the hit points window marks damage at 0, starts Unconscious at 0 and ends it on healing, each crossable; a dying character's turn starts with a reminder (`fight.follow`). Never decide a death: three failures and massive damage go to the DM.
- **The Use window** (app/feature_use.py, `_feature_window.html`, app.js "A feature's Use window"; the action widgets, roadmap 8; Brett chose A on `docs/mockups/use-window/`, Nov 13): a Battle row that costs something (`character_views.pays`) and has no roll of its own gets **Use** (`o.use_window`, set in `planned` for the character's own rows; Battle's `move`, the lists' `opt_row` and Search): its window says the feature's words and a plain **Use**, its cost a pill beside it as on the roll card (Brett chose the pill, Nov 14, `docs/mockups/healing-and-costs/`; `s.pill`, a checkbox `pay`): pressed, Use spends it through Use one's count with a sealed Undo (`feature-undo:`); unpressed, nothing is spent. A cost the rules make a choice starts unpressed, on the window and the roll card alike: `use.optional` in the rules book (Patient Defense's Disengage is free, its point buys the Dodge too; Step of the Wind; Deflect Attack's point only redirects), carried by `character_views.pays`. A row that rolls spends on the roll card instead; Wild Shape has Shift. A feature's rules-book `use` (app/data/rules/phb-2024.json, Lay On Hands) adds a pool (How many, starting at what the one it's for is missing), `for: "one"` (the party with their Hit Points) and `ways` (`heal: "chosen"`, `spend` and `ends` a condition; `row` is D&D Beyond's row that opens on that way). Healing or ending a condition on yourself, or as the DM, is made at once (`hit_points.make`, `item_use._end`); on another player's character the healing goes to Table rolls for them to apply (`table_rolls.add_roll` with `for_id`), as a potion given does, since a player changes only their own sheet; they're asked with a card that stays (**Healing given to someone**, below). tests/test_action_widgets.py, tests/browser/test_action_widgets.py. Next: Second Wind, Font of Magic and Channel Divinity's other options as data; then Cast, Hit points and the rest drawn by the same shell.
- **Every Battle's rows are ready the same way** (`character_views.planned(sheet, rows, scope)`; Brett, Nov 12: a Polar Bear's Unarmed Strike had no Grapple or Shove and no parts, "applies as globally as possible"): the character's own rows (`options`), a Wild Shape form's (`wild_shape.shaped`) and a creature's (`creatures.card`, `table_creatures.card`) all get their roll plan, dice at each level and parts from this one function, never by hand. `scope` (`roll_plan.SCOPES`) says whose extras apply (`roll_plan.adds_for`): "own" every feature and item, "form" class features and feats but not items, "creature" none of its master's; a stat block's own row is a natural weapon (`traits` "natural", never a Monk weapon) and its numbers source "block". Any row named Unarmed Strike (`roll_plan.unarmed`) offers Damage, Grapple and Shove; a form's DC is its own (`unarmed_dc` on the row, `character_views.unarmed_choices`; the server's Grapple or Shove roll reads the form too, `table_rolls._unarmed_row`). tests/test_roll_math.py walks every Battle of the party's sheets (own, each form, each creature): a row that rolls without a plan, parts that don't add up, or an Unarmed Strike without its three ways fails.

## Behind the scenes

- **Where a slow request's time went** (app/timing.py; Brett, Nov 12): every request carries a stopwatch (`Stopwatch`), and the steps that can wait add to it with `timing.step(…)` (a database connection, `get_db`; a character's lock, `ddb_bridge._lock`; reading D&D Beyond and storing a sheet, app/characters.py; a cache's reload); one over `SLOW` seconds logs a line on Railway with them, one over `REPORT` is a Sentry warning by route (numbers blanked, nothing else). Time a new step that can wait the same way. Railway's own HTTP logs (`railway logs --http --json`) say how long the server took and how many bytes arrived (`rxBytes`): the Nov 12 slowness was the extension's half-megabyte sheets piling up on a slow upload (the server took 7 ms; only that house's requests were slow), which the extension now sends one at a time per sheet and gzipped (0.12.2; `web.gunzip`, capped at `web.MAX_JSON_BYTES`). Every database read adds its time and a count to the request in hand (app/db.py's events on every `Engine`, `timing.read`: "database 1200 ms (40 queries)"), and `get_db` marks the route's start (`timing.started`): what came before it is "before the route" (a worker thread free, the sign-in check); both go into the weekly numbers too (Nov 23: askdnd.ai's pages took ten times what the same pages take locally, and nothing said where). A route that waits by design (the inbox held open, `/api/fft/inbox/wait`) times its wait as `timing.HELD`, which never counts towards slow (Nov 20: it was a slow request, and a Sentry warning, every 25 seconds).
- **Pages stay quick as the campaign grows** (Performance: the whole tool, Brett's Roadmap item, Nov 20): **On askdnd.ai** (app/speed.py, `RequestTime`): the stopwatch adds every request's time to its route's count for the day (the route as written, never an id; files, the health check and the live socket left out; a long-poll's held wait never counts), saved every 5 minutes by a loop the app starts; on Mondays the week's numbers are read, each route whose slowest tenth takes 800 ms or more (asked 20 times or more; estimated inside its time band, never the band's top: Nov 23, the top alone filed 25 routes, a 0.6 s page read as 1 s) becomes an issue under Behind the scenes › Speed, one per route kept up to date while open (an open one that wasn't slow that week gets a line saying so, with the week's numbers; rows saved with a campaign's name count as its page), and the run is the Scheduled job `speed` on Development; rows go after 60 days; `/api/fft/speed` gives Claude the week, with each route's average database time, reads and wait before the route (`RequestTime.db_ms`, `reads`, `before_ms`). **Locally:** `scripts/page_speed.py` times every main page and the calls pages repeat (as the admin, the DM and a player, on the local copy of askdnd.ai's data) with its database reads and the D&D Beyond sheets and summaries it loads, and estimates its time on Railway (each read ~2 ms more; each whole sheet ~540 KB, its summary ~170 KB, at ~4 MB/s).
