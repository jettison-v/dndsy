The flow

Inside a dev plan

Four points to agree

  1. Names. Feedback stays as where notes come in. Testing › Where it stands becomes Work: the ordered list of Product Areas and Roadmap items, each with a Start button and its plan. Testing keeps only the testing rounds. For you sits above all three and gathers everything waiting on you: decisions on a plan and checks to try.
  2. Where you test. On your Mac, before merge, as you do today. The cloud (staging or beta) only for what your Mac can't show: Google sign-in, several people on different devices, phones on the real site.
  3. The dev plan is a real thing in askdnd. It's the Product Area's existing batch, given a short written scope and a state: Scoping, Waiting on you, Building, Ready to test, Done. Your approvals attach to it and failed tests return to it. An area may hold Plans A, B and C. "C waits for B" comes later, once we need it.
  4. While a plan waits on your input, Claude doesn't start another plan. Triage keeps running, and nothing you'd need to look at gets built in the meantime. Two plans at once only through a second chat (the fresh-session trial).

Your draft, filled in

Claude · always running

Level 1 triage

  • Every issue gets its Product Area and Feature.
  • A note with several ideas is split into one issue each. The note itself is kept whole as shared evidence, pictures included, and nothing is deleted.
  • Duplicates are joined, and each issue gets a clear title.
  • Questions are answered. A question that will lead to a change becomes Something's wrong or An idea, or a new issue is opened from it.
  • Nothing waits on Claude more than 15 minutes (your pick 1A).
  • Triage never builds anything.
Anyone · any time

One-off feedback

  • A player's note waits in To approve until an admin files it. An admin's note is open straight away.
  • It goes into triage, then sits in its Product Area on the Work list until that area is started.
You start it

Structured development

  • You press Start on one Product Area or Roadmap item on the Work list. Start works only when Claude is free. Otherwise it says what Claude is on and offers to queue it next.
  • Claude first makes sure triage is finished for that area, then scopes it whole and writes the plan (or Plans A, B and C).
  • Mockups and questions go to For you. Claude stays on this plan until you've answered.
  • Build: code, tests, code review at high effort, the design check, every person and every place (CLAUDE.md › Before calling a change done).
  • Ready to test on your Mac, with each check tried by Claude first, and an email to you.
You start it

Testing round

  • You press Start round. The checks run page to page, and the round stays open until you press End round.
  • A failed check, or a note you send during the round, goes back into the same plan as a revision. A truly new idea becomes its own issue in triage.
  • Once every check passes: merge, deploy, and the plan is Done.

The pace rule. The speed comes from logging and grouping work, never from cutting steps. Every change gets the whole scope, the design system, code review and testing, however it arrived and however many came in at once.
