VTT Feature Map

What D&D Beyond's VTT does today, what people love in the others, and the smallest askdnd VTT that could replace it, with the full product behind it.

The short answer

D&D Beyond isn't as basic as it was

In 2026 it added shared 3D dice, scenes, journals, condition icons on tokens, and on October 30 Hit Point edits in its combat tracker.

The gap is the map's depth

Walls, line of sight, lighting and music are the clear holes. They're also what people outside D&D Beyond ask for most.

askdnd's edge isn't a map at all

Two-way sheets, worked-out Hit Points, Wild Shape, reminders and campaign memory. I found no other VTT with all of these for one table.

My recommendation: the MVP is one sentence: a whole session runs in askdnd, and nobody opens D&D Beyond's VTT. That means matching its map basics, not beating them. The features people love come after, in the order your table feels them.

One thing changes from the Simple VTT design: I'd left out the ruler, spell areas and fog. D&D Beyond has all three today, so a table would lose them by moving. They belong in the MVP.

What the community loves

There's no reliable ranking. I found no survey, and neither Foundry nor Owlbear publishes install counts on the pages I could read. This comes from forum threads, module pages and reviews, so read it as themes.

  • Line of sight is the most-named wish. Forum posts in December 2025 and February 2026 call it the reason they won't move to D&D Beyond's VTT. Foundry goes deepest: wall types, secret doors, windows, and light that spreads.
  • Combat that runs itself. Midi QoL rolls attacks, checks hits, applies damage and tracks Concentration in Foundry. People also turn much of it off and keep what they trust.
  • Starting fast. Owlbear Rodeo's appeal is drop a map, add tokens, play. AboveVTT is liked for one-click maps and tokens on top of D&D Beyond.
  • Keeping the D&D Beyond sheet. Beyond20, DDB Importer, dddice and AboveVTT all exist for this. It's a real need people patch together, and askdnd's bridge already does it both ways.
  • Dice that feel like dice. Dice So Nice is the example everyone gives. askdnd has this.
  • A DM layer. One Foundry user won't leave without a hidden map beneath the players' map, for secret doors. It's one voice, but it's cheap once there are map layers.
  • Paying once, or nothing. Foundry's one-time price and Owlbear's free tier come up often. Foundry's median user runs 19 modules, which shows both how much people want and how much setup they accept.

Watch this: D&D Beyond is building in the same spot

Its roadmap lists Non-VTT DM Tools as in active development, for running a game with or without a map. That overlaps the first step below, the board without a map. It doesn't change the plan, because that step sits on your bridge and reminders, but it's a reason not to spend long there. It also ships features quickly: condition icons arrived a month ago, so rows in the table above may change.

The final product, after the MVP

These are the same items as your VTT roadmap, grouped by what the table feels. The order after the MVP is a suggestion until a real session tells us more.

Fights that help more

Clickable stat blocks, attacks and saves the DM confirms, reactions at the right moment, and fights flowing into the recap. Roadmap 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 2.7.

A living map

Prepared scenes, walls, doors, line of sight, and imported maps that bring their walls along, which saves drawing them. A DM layer fits here. Roadmap 3.6, 3.7, 3.8.

Rules answers during play with page citations, and the map on a phone. Roadmap 5.1, 5.3.

What I wouldn't build

  • A module system or scripting. Foundry and Roll20 need these to serve everyone. askdnd serves one table, so it can simply build what you ask for.
  • Other game systems. Your rules are 2024 D&D only.
  • A character builder. D&D Beyond's sheet stays the sheet.
  • 3D maps or a marketplace. Nothing here points to your table needing them.

For you to decide

1. Is this MVP right?

It's bigger than the Simple VTT design because it adds the ruler, spell areas and fog.

My pick: yes. Otherwise the table loses tools when it moves.

2. Where do your maps come from?

D&D Beyond's book maps live there. Moving means uploading images the DM has the right to use. This is the biggest unknown, because I don't know which maps your table uses.

My pick: check the last few sessions' maps before step 3.

3. How far should automation go?

People love it, but your rule is remind, don't automate, and the DM makes every ruling.

My pick: keep Apply to in the MVP, and try DM-confirmed attacks only after real sessions.

4. Does one Table button work for you?

I looked at three places to launch the VTT: the menu bar, the fight band and the Live window. The menu bar is the only one always there.

My pick: the menu bar, with a Table card on the first page for new campaigns.

5. The Simple VTT questions still stand.

Who moves tokens, what players see of enemies, and where rolls go are answered there.
