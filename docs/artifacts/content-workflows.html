Who writes the campaign: an audit of askdnd's content workflows

You asked for the DM to own accurate content, helped by three inputs: their own writing, transcripts (most of the work) and players' corrections. This page maps how content gets in today, where the DM is left out, and what I'd change. The decisions at the bottom save as you answer.

Today

Three inputs, three different pipes, and the DM writes the least

What's missing

Seven gaps, most of them about the DM

GapTodayEffect
1. Nothing can be created by handNew people, places, factions, items and quests only come from a transcript or a data import. No "+ New" anywhere; no key moment or session without Claude.The DM can't prepare an NPC before the party meets them, or add something a transcript missed.
2. A transcript can overwrite the DM's wordsA suggested entry update replaces the whole description when accepted (Undo exists). Regenerate does the same.A hand-written description is one click from being lost, and nothing says who wrote it.
3. Two suggestion pipesEntry suggestions become facts (Merge · Reject). Recap and glossary suggestions go to an inbox (Use in redraft · Done · Dismiss).Different buttons for the same job; the facts list keeps growing.
4. Recaps belong to reviewersEditing recaps and key moments needs Reviewer access; the DM tick alone doesn't allow it.Works for your table (Mary has both), not for a DM who is only a DM.
5. No housekeepingNo change of kind, quest open or done, publish or hide, merge duplicates ("Capt Trager Grill" next to "Captain Trager Grill"), or delete.Transcript mistakes pile up and only an import can fix them.
6. Published recaps can't take a correctionA player's correction on a published recap shows under it; the recap text doesn't change unless a reviewer redrafts.The mistake stays in the story.
7. Players don't hear backAfter sending, a player sees "waiting"; nothing tells them it was used.Less reason to keep suggesting.
