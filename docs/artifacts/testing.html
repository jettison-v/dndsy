How askdnd is tested, and what the live test covers

askdnd has four kinds of test. Three run on GitHub and only stand in for D&D Beyond. The fourth, the live test, is the only one that uses the real thing: real Chrome windows on the Mac Mini, signed in as test players, playing real D&D Beyond sheets through askdnd on beta, the way your table does. It runs every night, after every deploy, and whenever you say "run the live tests".

The live test · where it runs

Four Chrome windows on the Mac Mini, driven like four people

The Mini stays on and signed in, so nobody has to watch and your MacBook can stay closed. A script, real_table.py, drives four copies of Chrome (the seats). It clicks and reads askdnd's pages on beta the way a person would, while each seat's askdnd bridge extension makes the changes on D&D Beyond. To decide whether a change really landed, the script never trusts askdnd's word: it reads the sheet's own record from D&D Beyond itself.

The live test · the seats

Test accounts and test characters, nothing of your table's

Each seat is its own person on D&D Beyond and in askdnd, all in the VTT test campaign on beta. Your personal D&D Beyond account is never a seat (automation could get an account flagged), and your table's real characters are never touched. Each sheet is chosen for what it exercises:

DM seat

WeslockePlayer

The DM of VTT test on D&D Beyond. Gives loot, opens the VTT for initiative, and makes every change itself when the players' sheets are closed ("through the DM").

Player 2

Cathartic, Cleric 9

The everyday sheet: hit points, conditions, death saves, Exhaustion, Heroic Inspiration, items and potions, prepared spells, giving and loot.

Player 3

Vi (test), Artificer 10

Slots, resources, coins and rests; the other end of a Give and of the Game Log; her Homunculus Servant for a creature's hit points. Not your own Vi.

Player 4

Levi, Druid 6 / Warlock 2

The hard cases: Wild Shape, two kinds of spellcasting (Druid slots and Pact Magic), a familiar, and initiative in the real VTT.

The seats also play twins: exact copies of your party's four characters (Cole, Trah, No Name and Big C, each named "(twin)") and one character for each class nobody at the table plays (Ranger, Bard, Rogue, Barbarian, Fighter), so every build at your table is tested without touching the real ones. Who plays which: docs/testing-setup.md.

The live test · when it runs

Three times it runs, two of them on their own

Every night
4:00, Mini time

All 45 runs (part 1), then every run that changes a sheet again through the DM, with every player's sheet closed. About 90 minutes. Catches what changed during the day on either side: an askdnd change, or D&D Beyond changing something.

After every deploy
Within 5 minutes

The Mini notices beta's version changed and runs four quick runs: closed-sheet, hit-points, condition and cast (22 checks, under 2 minutes). A deploy that breaks the table is known before anyone plays.

When you ask
"Run the live tests"

Claude runs part 1 and gives you the steps that still need a person (part 2). Worth asking after an extension update, after a change to the bridge or the fight, and the day before a session.

Each run tests what's on beta, never unmerged work. The nightly and the after-deploy check take turns with the seats, so they never collide.

The live test · one run

One run, step by step: hit-points

Every run follows the same pattern: do what a person would, then check in three places that it happened (askdnd's page at once, askdnd's confirmation, and D&D Beyond's own record), then put it back. Each check is one line of the result. This is a real run (Nov 5).

  1. Open the sheet. Player 2's seat opens Cathartic's D&D Beyond sheet, and askdnd's Live pill must say Connected.
  2. Take a hit in askdnd. On Cathartic's Battle page on beta, the seat presses − and takes 1 damage, as a player would.
  3. askdnd shows it at once. The Hit Points tile goes from 66 to 65 straight away, before D&D Beyond answers.
  4. askdnd confirms it. The extension made the change on the sheet and askdnd read it back: the tile stops saying it's on its way.
  5. D&D Beyond has it. The script reads the sheet's own record from D&D Beyond: 1 point of damage taken.
  6. Put it back. Healing in askdnd brings it back to 66, checked the same three ways.

The live test · how it looks after itself

A miss should mean askdnd, not the test

Real sheets keep state between runs: a spent slot or a Wild Shape form left behind makes the next run fail for the wrong reason. On Nov 5 one leftover made four later runs miss, so since then the test checks and tidies around every run, and a miss gets one more try before it counts.

The live test · its limits

What it covers, and what it doesn't

covered every night

  • Every kind of change askdnd makes on a sheet through the bridge, both from the player's own sheet and through the DM
  • That each change really lands on D&D Beyond, checked in D&D Beyond's own record, and is put back
  • The Live pill, the Game Log, and closing a sheet at awkward moments
  • Initiative in D&D Beyond's real VTT
  • The extension version actually in the store, as your players have it
  • D&D Beyond changing something overnight: it shows as a miss the next morning

not covered by the live test

  • The rest of a fight. Turns, conditions that end on a turn, a monster's damage with Apply to: D&D Beyond's VTT is a game canvas a script can't read reliably, so these are in part 2, by hand (about 15 minutes).
  • How things look. The run sees that a Game Log message passed, not how it reads; whether the 3D dice land right. Also part 2.
  • Phones and Safari. The seats are Chrome on a computer. Layout on an iPhone is checked by the browser tests (Safari's engine), not with real D&D Beyond.
  • Unmerged changes. It tests what's on beta. Changes are tried locally and by the faster tests first.
  • A real session. Five people, Zoom, an evening's worth of changes, with D&D Beyond busy. The nightly runs at 4:00, when D&D Beyond is quiet.
Results

Where results go, and what you'd see

A miss

A Sentry issue that alerts you

"Live test part 1: 3 missed (…)", or "… through the DM", or "Live test after deploying 98cb286". Say "check Sentry" and Claude reads it, finds the cause, and fixes askdnd or the test.

A flaky check

A Sentry warning

"… passed only on a second try": one issue that collects every time it happens, so its count shows how often.

The history

Every check, every night

Kept on the Mini. scripts/live_results.py lists the checks that didn't pass every time over two weeks, worst first, and the ones getting slower.

D&D Beyond drift

An email from GitHub

If a field the saved sheet copies have is gone or changed kind, the morning "D&D Beyond drift" run fails and GitHub emails you.
