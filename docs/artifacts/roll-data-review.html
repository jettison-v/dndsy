Roll Data Review

Before filling the engine from the whole 2024 Player's Handbook and Dungeon Master's Guide, a hard look at how its data is shaped. Today it's complete for the SRD and your table's sheets: 121 of the SRD's 125 spells with dice have a recipe (the other four are modifiers). But only 5 of the Player's Handbook's spells beyond the SRD do, 13 class features, 15 items and 12 modifiers, all chosen because someone at your table has them.

Built, Nov 10

Done
  • One shape, a file per book (app/data/rules/): moved with the party's plans checked identical, then class_rules' 47 riders moved in; riders and modifiers are one thing played one way, and what an add goes with is data. 20 riders that were only words now roll (Radiant Strikes, Rage Damage, Divine Strike, Colossus Slayer, Great Weapon Master…).
  • Player's Handbook: its spells beyond the SRD (read from your offline copy, mechanics only: 169 spell recipes, every die checked), class and subclass features that roll or add a number, species traits and feats.
  • Dungeon Master's Guide: weapons that add damage (a pill for that weapon only), wands and staffs that cast their spells with their own DC and charges, items with their own save. Most of its other dice are charges coming back at dawn or tables the DM rolls.
  • Left: books beyond these two are covered only where a sheet at your table has them (Xanathar's, Tasha's, Fizban's); a feature that only changes a rule (Evasion, Reliable Talent) stays words, as the DM rules.

Devil's advocate: what breaks at the scale of two books

1

Four ways to say "something is added to a roll"

Biggest

A rider in Python (Stunning Strike), a modifier in JSON (Sneak Attack), a spell event marked rider or on_hit (Hunter's Mark, Divine Smite), an item's use.rider (Thunderclap). Each has its own code path. It already shows: Radiant Strikes (Paladin 11, +1d8 Radiant on every melee hit) is a rider in words only, so it's said but never rolled, while Sneak Attack, the same kind of thing, rolls.

2

When a rider applies is read from its words

Fragile

"Once per turn, a hit with a Monk weapon or Unarmed Strike" is matched with patterns to decide weapon, Unarmed Strike or spell; "Monk weapon" needed its own special case. Hundreds of features would each be a new pattern to get right.

3

Rules written as code

Scale

89 class rules are Python. They can't be checked as a whole, generated from a book, or edited without touching code. Battle's notes (words for the page) sit beside mechanics (what rolls).

4

Scaling numbers need code

Scale

Each scaling feature gets a named token and a Python function ({sneak}, {lands_aid}). The PHB has dozens: Rage damage, Bardic Inspiration's die, Martial Arts, Brutal Strike, Channel Divinity…

5

Keyed by display names

Collisions

Curly apostrophes (Land’s Aid), suffixes stripped by hand (Hammer of Thunderbolts (Maul)), a Use an item: prefix, and a 2014 and a 2024 spell sharing one name. Every lookup has its own fallback.

6

Where it's from is in the file's layout

Provenance

Spells sit in spells, phb_spells, other_spells by book; items and features don't say. Two books' worth needs the book on every entry, so a table without a book never gets its rules.

7

One field means two things

Clarity

An event's on is both how it resolves (hit, save, auto) and what it does (heal, temp, plain), plus rider.

8

Items in two files

Duplication

What an item rolls is in the recipes (with use); what it gives on Battle is in item_actions.json. A magic item from the DMG would be written twice.

And against changing it

It works today

Every roll on the party's sheets plays its plan, and about 350 buttons are tested on every push. A restructure risks that.

Answer: the move is checked by the same tests plus a new one: every party plan is identical before and after.

It's more to learn

One general shape is more abstract than four specific ones.

Answer: four shapes are more to learn, and the general one is what makes homebrew a matter of picking values (your note).

Words still matter

The DM rules, so riders' words stay what the player reads.

Answer: every entry keeps its words (our own summary); structure decides only what the card offers and rolls.

Recommendation: one effect shape, one file per book

Recommended
  1. One entry per rule thing (spell, feature, feat, item, mastery) in a file per book: app/data/rules/srd-5.2.1.json, phb-2024.json, dmg-2024.json, other-books.json. Each entry names its book, its D&D Beyond ids where known, and how it was checked. Mechanics and our own summaries only: never book text.
  2. Two lists on every entry: rolls (what it rolls when used: resolve attack/save/none, effect damage/heal/temp/dice) and adds (what it does to another roll). Riders, modifiers, on-hit spells and item riders all become adds, played by one code path.
  3. When it applies is data: when (before, after the roll, after a failure, on a hit, on a miss, on damage, on damage taken), to (attack, save, check, damage), with (melee weapon, ranged weapon, Monk weapon, Finesse, Unarmed Strike, spell, a damage type), once (a turn), cost (a resource, a slot, a free use).
  4. Scaling is data: a few generic tokens (level:Class, mod:ability, pb, dc:ability) and a scale table on the entry ({"Druid": {"1": 2, "10": 3, "14": 4}}) in place of a Python function per feature.
  5. Battle's words stay in class_rules for now (attacks per action, notes, options): they aren't rolls. Their riders move to data.
  6. A schema and a test: every entry checked on every push; a coverage report over the books (not only the sheets) says what each book's entries roll and what's left in words.
{"id": "phb-2024:feature:paladin:radiant-strikes", "kind": "feature", "name": "Radiant Strikes",
 "class": "Paladin", "level": 11, "book": "phb-2024", "checked": "phb-2024",
 "says": "Every hit with a melee weapon or Unarmed Strike deals an extra 1d8 Radiant",
 "adds": [{"when": "on_hit", "to": ["damage"], "with": ["melee_weapon", "unarmed"],
           "amount": "1d8 radiant", "always": true}]}

Then the books

  1. Move today's data into the new shape with a converter; the party's plans must come out identical, and the every-roll test must pass.
  2. Player's Handbook: every spell beyond the SRD, every class and subclass feature and every feat that rolls or adds to a roll, checked against the offline copy.
  3. Dungeon Master's Guide: every magic item that rolls, asks for a save or adds to a roll.
  4. A coverage report over the books, and the every-roll test also plays a made-up character per class at levels 1, 5, 11 and 17, so the books' rules are played, not only listed.

Monsters stay as they are: their stat blocks are already data, read from the SRD and your Monster Manual.
