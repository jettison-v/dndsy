From table companion to virtual tabletop

How askdnd grows from today's recaps, character pages and table rolls into a full virtual tabletop for our table: live character state, combat, maps and tokens, the DM's media tools, and eventually voice and video. Each piece is built behind the VTT switch, tried in the test campaign, then switched on for the table.

Decided before this plan: it's for our table, without ruling out other groups later. The fight screen comes first (Brett, Oct 26), with hit points and the play log on it, then the rest of live character state. D&D Beyond stays where characters are built and leveled, and askdnd runs everything during play. Voice and video come later, as a nice to have. And every piece is built to scale and kept clean, even when that takes more effort.

Where we are · Oct 27

A strong base to build on

A lot of what a virtual tabletop needs already exists, and most of Phase 0 is done. The biggest gap is unchanged: D&D Beyond still owns hit points, spell slots and inventory during play, so askdnd can show them but not change them. The play state design was reviewed on Oct 26; next it shows on the fight screen, Phase 1.

The story

  • Recaps and key moments from notes and recordings
  • Campaign record: World, Quests, Relationships, Glossary, Map
  • DM tools, one inbox, and the DM's words protected
  • Version history with restore, nightly backups

Characters

  • D&D Beyond sync, every 2–3 minutes in Game Mode
  • Battle, Sheet, Plan, Spell Book, Wild Shape forms
  • Inventory and Features lists with pins and notes
  • Conditions, concentration and item effects like Invisible
  • A tour of every feature, on your own pages

The table

  • Table rolls on the server, on every screen within a second
  • Thrown 3D dice in each player's own set, tuned by admins
  • Several campaigns, roles per campaign, house rules
  • The VTT switch and a test campaign with test copies
  • Party: everyone's numbers, spells and items, filtered
What makes it better

What every piece should get right

These run through every phase. They're aimed at what tends to frustrate tables on D&D Beyond Maps, Foundry and Roll20, and at our own habits. Your own list of frustrations will sharpen them (see the end).

One place, not five tabsSheet, map, story, rules and media together, instead of Zoom, D&D Beyond's sheet, its map, askdnd and MacWhisper side by side.
Automation the DM can overruleA roll compares to AC and damage applies itself, but the DM sees it first and can change it. The DM makes every ruling.
Nothing changes silentlyEvery hit point, slot, item and move is in a play log with who did it, and anything can be undone.
No setup taxNothing to install, host or keep updated, and no add-on hunting. It opens in a browser and works.
The story remembers itselfFights, loot and reveals flow into the session's recap, so nobody takes notes and transcripts matter less.
Remind, don't over-automateaskdnd keeps the numbers and reminds the table of the check that follows (a Concentration save and its DC, death saves at 0 HP). People roll and decide; a reminder becomes automatic only after playtests show the table wants it.
Inform, don't adviseShow what you can do and how it works at the right moment. Never tell anyone what to do.
Built to scale, kept cleanEach piece is built to hold up with more copies of the app, more tables and more data, and whatever a change makes unnecessary is removed in the same change.
How it's built · added Oct 24

Five foundations every phase stands on

Each is built once, in Phase 0, and every later item uses it rather than inventing its own. Reworking the live connection showed why: a shortcut in a foundation turns into rework in everything built on it.

Live connection

Done

Every open page keeps a connection to its campaign. A change goes through Postgres to every copy of the app and every screen at the table, within a second. It says only what changed; the page fetches it the usual way, which decides who sees what.

Rule: every item that changes something the table sees announces it. For maps, token moves will carry their position, and hidden monsters go on a DM-only channel.

Play state and the play log

Designed · reviewed Oct 26

One home for what askdnd owns during play (HP, slots, resources, conditions, Wild Shape, item counts), per character per campaign. Every change is a log entry with who, what and why, and Undo applies the reverse. D&D Beyond refreshes never overwrite play state; where they meet, the rule is written per field.

Replaces today's three scattered fields (Wild Shape, concentration, conditions), which don't reach other screens yet. Two people editing at once can't overwrite each other. It shows on the fight screen (Phase 1).

Files

Built · old copies to remove

Maps, handouts and tokens in private storage (S3), served through askdnd with the same access checks, cached, with upload limits.

Today's pictures are in storage; their old database copies go once beta's copy is checked. Needed before maps (Phase 3) and Show to the table (4.1).

Rules content as data

Done

The free 2024 rules content (SRD 5.2) as data; the DM's own creations and anything from purchased books in the database, never in the code.

Stat blocks, attacks that resolve, and rules answers in play all read from it.

Testing the table, and knowing when it breaks

Done

Pages now update each other live, which server tests can't see: a few browser tests drive a player's page and the DM's at once on every change. And errors on beta reach you before the table finds them mid-game.
