What to make into components

A scan of askdnd's pages for the pieces that repeat, which of them are worth making into one shared part, and in what order. You asked after the Hunter's Mark fix: the roll sequence first, and what else.

My recommendation: three components, in this order.

1. The action roll (everything from pressing an attack to the damage landing on the target), 2. the action window (Cast, Use one, Rest, Give and the other eight windows that change a character), and 3. one set of pills and chips. Everything else is already shared or isn't worth the churn. Each one gets a page on Manage › askdnd › Design system, which becomes the component library.

What "a component" means in askdnd

One piece of the page, defined once, used everywhere it appears. In askdnd's stack that's four parts kept together: a template macro (what it draws), its styles, its bit of script (what it does when pressed), and the Python that decides what it shows. Today some pieces already work that way (the corner cards, the side panel, the list with filters). Others were built one at a time, so the same idea exists in several slightly different copies. Hunter's Mark is the example: a rule about extra damage on a hit had nowhere to live, because no single place owns "what happens when you attack".

The biggest win isn't tidier code. It's that the rules move into Python, where they can be tested. The roll window today is assembled from text inside the browser script, so a rule like "add Hunter's Mark to each hit" can only be checked by clicking.

The candidates, most valuable first

1

The action roll

Do first
What it is
The whole sequence after you press an action: the roll window (Normal, Advantage, Disadvantage and why; who sees it; added dice), the dice, the result, then what follows: damage, reroll, add a die, Apply to the target, the target's Concentration save, a death save's mark, Hidden ending.
Today
About 450 lines of browser script build the window and result from text, deciding by the roll's kind and whether it's a follow-up. Each follow-up opens a fresh window. Riders (Hunter's Mark, Sneak Attack, Stunning Strike, Divine Smite) aren't part of it; they're a list under the columns you add up yourself.
As a component
The server describes each action's roll as a short roll plan: its steps (to hit, damage, a save), what can be added at each step and why (riders with their cost and when they apply), and what can follow. One component draws any plan. New rules become new entries in the plan, written in Python and tested, and every place that rolls gets them at once.
Why first
It's the most-used flow at the table, and the next fixes you've asked for (Hunter's Mark in the damage, riders after a hit) need it. That's why the roll workflow mockups come with this scan: pick the flow, then build the component around it.
Size
Large: about two sessions, plus moving the browser tests that cover rolling.
2

The action window

Next
What it is
The small window that changes a character: Cast, Use one and Give one back, Change coins, Give, Rest, Shift (Wild Shape), Ready, Prepare, Inspiration, using an item, and Hit Points.
Today
Twelve templates with the same shape (a title, what's left, a choice or two, a warning when the sheet can't be reached, buttons), and twelve server routes that each check the sheet can be reached, make the change, send it to D&D Beyond and offer Undo. Undo is done three different ways.
As a component
One window shell and one server helper for "an action": check, change, send, and an Undo that works the same everywhere. A new action is then a few lines saying what it changes.
Why second
Cast already hands off to the roll ("Cast and roll"), so the two should meet cleanly. It also makes "say it where it starts" (the warning when D&D Beyond can't be reached) impossible to forget.
Size
Medium: one session.
3

Pills and chips

Next, small
What it is
The small rounded labels and buttons: tags, conditions, spell chips, Wild Shape forms, roll chips, list filters, Concentration, the Part of link.
Today
About ten separate styles, each with its own size, corners and colours. That's why the Part of chip went oval (feedback 35) and why sizes drift from page to page.
As a component
One pill with a few variants: a label, a button, a toggle, and a condition with its colour and icon. Same height, corners and wrapping everywhere.
Size
Small: half a session, mostly styles. Low risk, and the layout tests at every width catch mistakes.
4

Pop-overs and folds

Later
What it is
Things that open in place: + Condition, a condition's pill, Concentration's pop-up, the table's ⋯ and Map ▾ menus, the phone folds (Resources, Wild Shape, pinned spells).
Today
Five ways of opening and closing, each with its own rule about clicks outside and Esc. The ⋯ menu that didn't open on the table was one of these.
As a component
One "opens in place" behaviour: a click outside or Esc closes it, the page waits to refresh while it's open, and it remembers itself on a phone where that matters.
Size
Small to medium. Worth doing once the action roll settles, since the roll window is one of these too.
5

Number tiles and rows

Already shared: keep
What it is
Hit Points, AC, Speed and the other tiles (37 uses), and the rows on Battle and Sheet.
Today
Already macros used everywhere (ntile, opt_row, ri), with D&D Beyond's sync marks built in. They work.
Recommendation
Leave them. Add each to the Design system page so they're documented with the rest.
6

Side panel, modal, corner cards, lists, pictures

Already shared: keep
Today
Each is already one piece: the side panel and modal (one way to open, one way to refresh), corner cards (the one place for messages), the list with filters (ten pages), and the one Picture window. These are what the other components should look like when they're done.
Recommendation
Leave them. Document them on the Design system page.

How I'd do it

  1. You pick the roll flow from the mockups (the other page). That decides what the action roll component has to do.
  2. The roll plan in Python, first: every action on Battle gets its plan (steps, what can be added and why, what follows), tested against the party's four sheets the way Battle's columns are today. No visible change yet.
  3. One component draws it, in the flow you picked, replacing the roll window and its follow-ups. Hunter's Mark, Hex, Sneak Attack and the other riders appear where the flow puts them. The browser tests that roll move over and grow.
  4. The Design system page becomes the library: each component with a live example and where it's used, starting with the action roll.
  5. Then the action window, then pills, each a pull request of its own, tried on your local copy and on prod's VTT test like everything else.

This would slot in before the rest of askdnd's own table, since the table's fight uses the same rolls.
