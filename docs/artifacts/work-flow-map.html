The map

Every path, start to live

Seven stages, left to right. Things come in on the left. After Claude builds it, each plan takes its lane: Ship along the top, Look in the middle, Careful below. Arrows going back are the loops: a question for you, something wrong, a test you can't do yet.

On a phone the map scrolls sideways. The numbers on the map match the steps below.

The lanes

How much testing a change needs

Each plan gets one lane, picked by rules from what it touches. You can tap a plan up a lane; Claude can move one up too, saying why; down only on your word. In every lane you test a thing once at most.

Ship

Nothing a person would notice, or a fix to a known error: work behind the scenes, tests, dependency updates, a fix for an error Sentry caught, a word changed.

Nothing. It's on Work's Done list if you'd like to read it.

Tests and the full code review, merged, then Claude checks it on askdnd.ai.

Look

Something people see, within one Product Area. Most fixes and polish.

Try the batch once, on askdnd.ai, when it suits you.

Tests, review, pictures before and after, and Claude tries it first. Merged, behind a Labs switch if it's big. Anything wrong is undone the same day, or fixed.

Careful

Who sees what, signing in, spending, the D&D Beyond bridge and extension, and database changes that alter or remove data.

One round on staging. You don't test it again on askdnd.ai.

One round branch on staging, Claude tries it there first. Passed: it merges exactly as tested, in one deploy, then automatic checks on askdnd.ai (Claude, the Mac Mini, a live D&D Beyond run where it applies).

The dial: askdnd is at Start

Each check is a setting per lane and level, so the same tools could come with you to another company and start light there too. Team (a second builder, a second table or DM, about 15 regular users) adds staging for Look work, release notes and a second tester. Company (paying customers, people building alongside Claude) adds approval by someone else. askdnd would notice a trigger and tell you; you decide. The detail is on Work Flow, Sized to Grow.

Your time: about 2 hours a week now, 1 hour later

Careful rounds come first in For you, each with its minutes. Look batches wait for when you have time, and that's safe because they're already live and Claude tried them first. Ship needs none. At most two plans wait on you at once. Once you're working full time, Look batches will simply wait a little longer.

What makes it move

Who has to be there, and when

The map only moves when someone's there to move it. Here's what that means in practice, so nothing on it promises more than it does.

Claude moves only while a chat is open

Claude does its work in a chat in the desktop app. With one open, it listens to askdnd and works down the list above the cut line on its own. With none open, nothing moves, and Work says so plainly: "Claude isn't listening". Starting Claude on a schedule waits for the Team level.

Claude hears askdnd between steps

A note or an answer reaches Claude when it finishes what it's doing: a few minutes, sometimes longer in the middle of a big build. It's never lost. The end-of-turn check won't let Claude stop while anything said to it has no reply.

Your answers reach Claude by themselves

An answer you tap in For you, or picks you save on a mockup in askdnd, land in Claude's inbox. Comments on a claude.ai page like this one don't: tell Claude in chat when you're done, and it reads them all together.

Never waiting on each other

While a plan waits on you, Claude goes on to the next row above the line, on its own branch. Ship and Look keep merging while you test. Only a Careful round holds merges, and an idle round ends itself after 12 hours.

Built so a Roadmap can follow

A Roadmap feature isn't scoped yet. When it comes, it should only add views (an item's page, its progress, what shipped, its linked ideas, players weighing in), never rework what's built here. Three rules in these slices keep it that way:

  • Order, the cut line, the lane and the stage belong to the plan, not the Roadmap item. Claude takes plans, so Work orders plans; an item groups its plans. One order only, never a second list on a Roadmap page.
  • A Roadmap item can hold many plans over time (phase 1 now, phase 2 later, ideas parked for a later phase).
  • An item's status is worked out from its plans, never kept by hand. Whose turn it is moves onto the plan as its stage; Work doesn't use the item's hand-set now, next or later and next step.

Linking ideas and issues to an item already fits: they hang off the item, and its plan answers each one.

Where it runs

Three places, one job each

You test a thing once at most: Careful work on staging, Look work on askdnd.ai after it's live. Your Mac is Claude's workbench and never yours to keep fresh.

Inside "Claude builds it"

Every plan, the same steps

However a change arrived, whichever lane it takes. The lanes change how much of your time it needs, never these steps.

  1. Read it wholeEvery open issue and failed check in it together, its part of docs/MAP.md and the decisions behind it.
  2. Write the planOne fix for all of it, not one per note, and its lane. A design choice becomes a question or a mockup in For you.
  3. Build, one fix at a timeReproduce it, fix it from the design system, add a test that fails without the fix. Database changes only add.
  4. Look at itIn a browser on a laptop and a phone, for every person (player, DM, admin, View as), every place and every state.
  5. Review it allThe code review at high effort, pictures of every main page before and after, the full test suite.
  6. Checks, tried firstEach check written one action a step and tried by Claude before it reaches you: on staging for Careful work, before merging for Look.
