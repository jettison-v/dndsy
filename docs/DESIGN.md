# askdnd Design Guide

_Last updated 2026-10-31 · Applies to every page in the app. The living component page (**Manage › Design system**, `/manage/design`) shows every token and component with the app's own styles; old mockups are archived in `docs/mockups/archive/`._

askdnd is read before a session and reviewed after one. People open it on a laptop next to Zoom and D&D Beyond, sometimes on a phone. It should feel like a well-kept campaign journal, not an admin panel.

## Principles

1. **Read first, edit on demand.** Saved content shows as finished text. Editing is a deliberate step (an **Edit** link), with **Save** and **Cancel**, and returns to the reading view. A form is never the resting state.
2. **Only what needs a decision stays open.** Once a question is answered or skipped, or a suggestion is accepted or rejected, it moves into a collapsed **Done (n)** drawer at the bottom of its section. Everything in Done can still be opened, edited or undone.
3. **One primary action per step.** Each section has at most one red button. Everything else is a quiet secondary button or a text link.
4. **Status is always visible**, in the same place and the same colors everywhere (Draft, Published, Edited since publishing, Accepted…).
5. **Say what happens next.** Buttons are verbs with outcomes ("Publish to the table", "Redraft"), and anything that costs money or is seen by the table says so in a short line beneath.
6. **Readable first.** Normal-case text everywhere; all-caps only for the logo and divider labels. Story text is askdnd's own type on a faint scroll, at a comfortable line length (Brett, 2026-11-05).
7. **Calm and roomy.** Generous padding, few divider lines, color only for status and the one primary action.
8. **Works on a phone.** One column on phones (640px and below), tap targets 44px, and the publish action pinned to the bottom of the screen.
9. **Never lose work.** Cancel discards only that edit; a changed published recap shows "Edited since publishing" until republished.
10. **Inform, don't advise** (from the product rules). Copy explains what something is or does. It never tells players what to do in the game.

## Review workflow (admin)

The session review page is a short checklist, top to bottom:

1. **Claude's questions.** Answer or skip. A "Redraft" prompt appears once there are new answers.
2. **Recap.** Read; edit if needed.
3. **Key moments.** Read the list; edit, retype, delete or add individual events inline.
4. **Suggested updates.** People, places and spellings. Accept, edit, or reject; undo later from Done.
5. **Publish** from the progress panel (sidebar on desktop, bottom bar on phones).

## Visual system

Dark only (Brett, 2026-10-22), inspired by D&D Beyond (dark chrome, red accents, warm parchment-toned story text) without copying its logo, name or artwork. Every value below is a token in `app/static/app.css` `:root`; pages use the names, never the values. `tests/test_design_system.py` fails on a text size, color or screen width outside the tokens. Sizes in `em` are allowed (they scale a parent that uses a token).

**Colors**

| Token | Use |
|---|---|
| `--bg` · `--surface` · `--surface-2` | Page · sections and panels · buttons, inputs, nested blocks |
| `--line` · `--line-strong` · `--chrome` | Dividers and borders · stronger rules, swatch edges · the top bar |
| `--text` · `--text-strong` · `--muted` · `--faint` · `--link` | Body · white on red and headings that need it · secondary · tertiary (lightened Nov 13, the design grade's fix 1: 5.2 : 1 on the page, 4.6 on a surface, past the 4.5 small text needs) · links |
| `--story-bg` · `--story-line` · `--story-text` | Answers, proposed text: warm "parchment at night"; `--story-text` is also story text's colour |
| `--parch-1..3` · `--rod-1..3` · `--story-cap` · `--story-fleuron` | The faint scroll behind story text (Brett, 2026-11-05, Umber · Inter · Faint): its parchment, a recap's rods, first letter and fleuron |
| `--red` (`--red-hover`, `--red-soft`) | The one primary button, the top-bar rule, focus |
| `--amber` · `--green` · `--blue` (each with `-soft`) | Draft or needs attention · published, done · edited since publishing, new |
| `--violet` (`--violet-soft`) | Reminders (the bell) |
| `--k-action` · `--k-bonus` · `--k-reaction` · `--k-other` | The action economy, the only colored headings (see Section headings) |
| `--cond-*` (`--cond-poisoned`…, `--cond-dark`, `--cond-dark-edge`) | Each condition's own color (its pill, emblem, Party icon, menu bar dot and the page's edge), by its `[data-cond]` slug |

Status colors are text on their `-soft` tint.

**Text sizes** (Brett, 2026-10-22): `--fs-xs` 12px labels, secondary lines and tags · `--fs-sm` 14px list cells, chips, controls · `--fs-md` 16px body and a row's name · `--fs-lg` 19px section headings · `--fs-xl` 26px the character's name · `--fs-2xl` 30px page titles. UI in Inter at 16px / 1.6; story text in Inter too, at `--fs-md` / 1.7 on its faint scroll.

**Fonts** (Brett, 2026-11-13: "limit the number of fonts used to the ones we have selected"): three, each a token in app.css's `:root`, and nothing else on any page.

| Token | Font | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `--sans` | **Inter** (fallback system-ui) | Everything: the UI, story text, recaps, quoted answers, the DM's read-aloud text, notes |
| `--cond` | **Roboto Condensed** 700 | The logo, and small uppercase labels (a section's or column's label, a roll card's kind, Manage's groups) |
| `--tale-cap` | **Cormorant Garamond** 500 | The tale's flourishes only: a recap's first letter, a chapter's title, an award's line and quote mark |

The serif quoted answers used (Iowan Old Style, Palatino) was hard to read and is gone. A new place for text takes one of these by its use, never a font of its own; tests/test_design_system.py fails on any other font in app.css, a page or app.js, and on a Google Fonts link that loads anything else. Mockups use the same three. An email names Inter with the system's fonts after it, since it can't load web fonts.

**Space:** a 4px scale, `--sp-1` 4 · `--sp-2` 8 · `--sp-3` 12 · `--sp-4` 16 · `--sp-5` 24 · `--sp-6` 32. Prefer `gap` on flex and grid over margins between siblings. **Battle's blocks** (numbers, Wild Shape, the Status row, the spell bar, the columns) stack in `#battle-body` with one `--sp-4` gap and no margins of their own (Brett, 2026-10-29: the Status row sat closer to the spell bar than to Wild Shape). Where a block's parent has no gap, a **spacing helper** on the same scale, never an inline style (2026-10-27; `tests/test_design_system.py` checks every page): `.mt-1`…`.mt-5` space above, `.mb-1`…`.mb-3` below, `.m-0` none, `.byline` a note tucked under what it's about (who added it and when), `.push` a button at the far end of its row, `.grow` the part of a row that takes the room left, `.row.tight` small controls that belong together. Small component rules took the rest: `.btn.block` (a card's one action), `label.field.short`, `.callout.ready`, `.title-action`.

**Corners:** `--r-xs` 3px tiny marks (a pip, a bar) · `--r-sm` 6px small chips, menu bar controls and inner boxes · `--r-md` 8px buttons, inputs and number tiles · `--r-lg` 12px sections, cards, panels, windows · `--r-pill` tags, chips, counts · `50%` round things. The test fails on any other corner.

**Shadows:** `--lift` for anything that floats over the page (a menu, a window, a card); the test fails on a near-copy of it. A side panel's shadow falls to the left; a coloured glow belongs to its component.

**Controls:** search boxes, every text, number and date field, selects and regular buttons are `--control-h` tall (32px on desktop, 44px on phones; Brett, 2026-10-22), so a field and the button beside it always match; small buttons `--control-h-sm` (26 / 36px); chips `--chip-h` (28 / 36px); everything in the menu bar `--bar-h` (36 / 44px). A file picker's button looks like a regular button. Fields use 16px text on phones so iPhones don't zoom in.

**The page's scrollbar** (a computer): a 12px lane that's always there, so a page never shifts sideways, drawn as part of the page: the menu bar and its red line run through it to the window's edge (Brett, 2026-10-31). Phones keep their own.

**Tap targets on a phone** (tests/browser/test_layout.py): at least 36px, the regular controls 44px. What sits among words (a pin, a bell, + Add) keeps its size and takes a larger area around it (`::after`).

**Panels and modals** (three sizes, chosen by what's inside): side panel `--panel-w` 420px (details for one thing; also inside a modal) · modal `--modal-w` 720px (a form or short content) · large `--modal-lg-w` 1180px (rich content in columns: a Wild Shape form, a combo, a leveling plan, the Spell Book). On phones each is a sheet from the bottom. (A full-window size for the Spell Book was retired on 2026-10-29: too wide on large screens.)

**Screen widths:** phone ≤ 640px, tablet ≤ 900px, wide ≥ 1100px, and the full menu bar > 1240px (below 1100px the Live pill's long words shorten, Navigation) (media queries can't use variables; the test allows only these). A list's own width (container queries) is its columns' need instead; see Narrow screens.

- **Key-moment types** are colored words, not boxes: Event (muted), Reveal (lavender), Decision (blue), Loot (amber), Theory (italic, muted) so speculation never looks like fact.

## Modes: Table and Backstage (Nov 20)

One design system, two modes (Brett's picks on Backstage Mode, `docs/mockups/backstage-mode/`: "too playful for a tool used by engineering, QA and product people"). The pieces are the same; a mode only changes the tokens' values and how lists lay out.

- **Table mode** is everything the table sees (players' and the DM's pages): as the rest of this document describes.
- **Backstage mode** is every Backstage page (`main.manage-page`) and the windows opened over it, on a laptop and wider (901px+; a phone or tablet keeps Table mode's touch sizes and 16px boxes): type 11.5 · 12.5 · 13.5 · 15 · 19 · 22px, tighter space and controls (28px), tabular numbers, one font with labels in plain words (no gold, no condensed capitals), and the whole window's width up to 2,200px, a paragraph still stopping at about 75 characters. app.css › "Backstage mode"; tests/browser/test_backstage_mode.py.
- **Lists in Backstage mode are tables** (`.dtable` in a `.table-scroll`, Backstage › Global › Design system › Table): a header row, columns sized by their header's width class (`dt-w1`…`dt-w5`), the title the one cell that wraps, groups as divider rows; a row of one item opens it in the one item window.
- **One item, one window**: in Backstage, a link or a row of one item (an issue, a check, a note, a question) opens it in the item window, the side panel at the right (`data-panel-url="/manage/item/<key>?from=<its list>"`), where it's read and acted on; ↑ ↓ walk its list, a press moves on to the next. A link to a group (a plan, a Product Area) goes to its page.
- **A row that stands for a group goes to its page** (a plan on Work: `tr[data-href]`, app.js), where its parts are tabs (a plan's Overview, Issues, Test round, Timeline); a row of one item opens the item window. A table's sizes come only from its width classes (`dt-w0` 40px for a grip or a ⋯, then `dt-w1`…`dt-w5`); a line Claude works down to is a `tr.dt-cut`.
- **An issue's kind** (Nov 20): `kind_word`, Fix in red or Improvement in violet, the same in every list and window.
- **Short titles** for issues and checks: the part of askdnd first, then what's wrong ("Check buttons: Passed looks already chosen"), about 45 characters; the reporter's words stay whole on the issue.

## Rules for every page (Brett's reviews, 2026-10-23)

Read these before designing or building any page; sessions that skipped them produced rework.

- **Mock up first** (`docs/mockups/`; the table's own characters, "Enemy 1" and "NPC 1" for the rest, Brett 2026-10-26), get Brett's OK, then build one piece at a time. Mockups show phone and an in-between width (about 1000px), not just desktop.
- **Reuse the components below; no one-off layouts or inline spacing.** If something new is needed, add it to the
  design system (and Manage › Design system) first.
- **Full width.** Pages use their standard width (`.page`, or `.page.wide` for Campaign, Party, Help). No
  narrow reading columns on their own.
- **One tab style:** the underline tabs (`.tabbar`) on the page itself, as on Campaign. Never a band wider than the content. On a phone a row that doesn't fit scrolls sideways with the open tab always in view, and fades the edge it's cut at (`.cut-l`, `.cut-r`; Brett 2026-11-02, the UI pass).
- **A page's own action** (`.title-row > .title-action`) stays at the right end of the title row at every width, a secondary button; the title wraps beside it. Red stays for the one next step on a page. Page titles are 26px on phones (Brett 2026-11-02, the UI pass).
- **Chips are blue when on**, everywhere: filters, History's Show, the shared lists (Brett 2026-11-02, the UI pass).
- **A reader's sections** (`_admin_bits.html` `rd_section`, `.rd-sec`; Brett's pick A, Nov 18, `docs/mockups/fft-reader/`, issue 4: "it all just blends together"): a reader's parts as cards, each a small label with its count over what's in it; the part saying where something stands has its left edge in its state's colour; History folds with its count. A conversation's lines say when (`data-clock="when"`: the time today, Yesterday, else the day) and what came since you last looked is marked New (issue 32).
- **A chip you can take away** (`.chip.chip-pair`: `.chip-name` and `.chip-x`, each its own button, one pill with a hairline between; Brett's round, Nov 18, issue 21): the name picks it, the ✕ takes it away, its aria-label naming what. Send a note's pictures you added; never on something that must stay (the page's own picture).
- **Lists get filters.** Anything people browse (names, spells, items, quests) has search and chip filters with live
  counts, not just a scroll.
- **Size to the content.** Diagrams, windows and cards are as big as what's in them; no large empty frames.
- **Quiet actions.** One primary (red) button per screen; repeated per-item actions (Edit, Mark done) are small
  secondary buttons.
- **Buttons say where they go** in plain words ("More about Alea"), not how the app works ("Read in World").
- **Terse game words** ("1st-level", never "1st-level slot"); players know the terms.
- **No code words on a page.** A file name, a class or a roadmap phase belongs in the docs, not in text a DM or a player reads. Nor monospace: the page's own fonts (a key to press is a `<kbd>`).
- **One way to write each thing** (the UI review, Nov 16): money in dollars and cents through the `money` filter ("$0.13", "under $0.01"; never "13¢"); a date "Nov 5", with the year when it isn't this one or a list spans years ("Nov 5, 2026"); a time "3:00 AM"; a session "Session 29 · Feb 7, 2026" (`session_label`; a key moment's slot "Session 13 · Nov 6").
- **A page only some can see says so** in its subtitle: every admin page ends "Only admins can see this page."; a campaign page names who ("only the DM, reviewers and admins see this page").
- **A form a page's own action opens is a window** (`data-modal-url`, its form in the window's body with Cancel): + Add to the table, + Add an account, + New campaign. Remove is always the quiet red link (`.btn.link.danger`), asking first (`data-confirm`, `hx-confirm`, or a question that opens under it).
- **Check every width** (phone, tablet, about 1000px, wide) and every role (admin, DM, player; Manage › View as) before
  calling it done. The width check (tests/browser/test_layout.py) does the measurable part on every pull request; see
  Narrow screens below for what it checks and how each part of a page gives way.

## Narrow screens (2026-10-26)

Pages are used from 375px phones to wide monitors, and the in-between widths (a laptop with a side panel, a half-screen
window) are where things break. The rules:

- **A row's parts are as wide as their words, and side-by-side words share a line** (Brett, Nov 19: "alignment is off
  again … that seems to happen a lot"). Never equal shares (`flex: 1`) for parts whose words differ: a part breaks onto
  two lines only when the row truly has no room (the check's `squeezed`). A bold label beside a smaller note lines up on
  their baselines (`align-items: baseline`; a control in the row `align-self: center`), never centred as boxes (`off
  baseline`, a rule since the Live UI Audit, Nov 19; an icon with no words, ✕ or ?, is centred on purpose and left out).
- **Nothing scrolls sideways.** The page is never wider than the window. A wide table scrolls inside its own box, or
  (better) switches to its compact layout: a record table (`table.list.stacked`) becomes one block per row on a phone,
  its first cell whole, the rest after it on a wrapping line, each named by its `data-label` when the value alone
  doesn't say (Tokens, Cost), the buttons last (the UI review, Nov 16: Usage's calls, Sessions, the bridge's changes,
  Backups' imports).
- **A title never breaks inside a word** (Nov 16): beside the title row's action it grows from its narrowest word, and
  only when even that can't fit does the action go under it, at the right. A box's hint (placeholder) fits the box, and
  a chart's words are the page's own text over the drawing, never drawn inside it to scale with it.
- **One-line controls stay on one line.** Buttons, tabs, chips, tags and the menu bar's controls never break onto two The one exception (Brett, 2026-10-30): inside anything smaller than a page (the roll window, a side panel, a modal, a corner card) a long label wraps inside its button, so it never runs out of the box.
  lines (`.btn` is `nowrap`); the row around them wraps instead.
- **Text stays in its box.** Menus, panels, cards and buttons hold their text; long words (a web address) break
  (`overflow-wrap` on `body`), and names that must stay on one line end in "…".
- **Decide what gives way, in order, for anything that shares a row.** Write it next to the component's CSS.
- **Boxes you type in are at least 16px on a phone or any touch screen** (Nov 8). Safari on an iPhone or iPad zooms the
  whole page in when you tap a smaller one and leaves it zoomed. One rule in app.css sets the floor for every field,
  whatever its own class says; the layout check calls a smaller one "zooms".

## Components

Each one is on Manage › Design system with its class or macro. Use these; don't invent a near-copy.

- **Buttons** (`.btn`): four styles, **Primary** (`.primary`, red, one per screen), **Secondary** (plain `.btn`), **Quiet** (`.link`, text only) and **Danger** (`.link.danger`, always confirms), each in two sizes (regular and `.small`). Labels are verbs with outcomes. **Row action** (`.row-act`; Brett, 2026-10-29): an action inside a list's row (the Spell Book's Roll, and Concentrate as its ◎ icon with a label on hover) is a compact pill, so the row keeps its room for the columns; amber on hover when it rolls. In a panel a lone Primary spans it (`.panel .btn.primary`); beside other buttons in a row (`.row`, `.act-bar`) it's their size, every button in the row one height (issue 57, Nov 20).
- **Pills**, four kinds (Brett, 2026-10-22): **Tag** (`.tag`) labels something and is never clickable (Conc., Ritual, Draft); a row's tags and its cost are one run after its name (`.tag-run`) that wraps as a whole, on Battle and Sheet alike; **Chip** is clickable, one look with a blue on state (filters, Wild Shape forms, spells to cast, conditions, the roll window's choices: `.ls-chip`, `.ws-chip`, `.spell-chip`, `.cond-pill`, `.chip`), `--chip-h` tall; **Count** (`_lists.html` `count_pill(n)`, `.count`; Brett's pick A, Nov 17, `docs/mockups/counts-and-marks/`) is a number by a tab, heading or See all link, drawn as a small rounded pill a little bolder than its words (a tint of its words' colour in a link or a heading, blue on the chosen tab; in a See all link it follows the underlined words, with no arrow: Brett, Nov 17), always through `count_pill`, never a hand-made `<span class="count">` (tests/test_design_system.py) (every tab that holds a list counts it: Campaign's Sessions, World, Quests and Glossary), never "(17)" in a link's words, and a 0 isn't shown (a count means there's something there; Brett's pick 4, Nov 16); **Roll chip** (`.roll-chip`, `.roll-hit`) rolls dice and turns amber on hover. **A chip's own chips** (`.fft-feat-tray`, Brett's comment on `docs/mockups/product-taxonomy/`, Nov 18): when pressing a chip opens a second level (a Product Area's Features on Feedback), they hang under it as a tray, indented with a blue line down its left and named for the chip ("Battle's features ›"), so they never read as the first row wrapping. ## Implementation notes

- Server-rendered Jinja templates; small interactions (Edit/Cancel, answer, accept, undo) use HTMX partial swaps so the page doesn't jump.
- A press never reloads the page (the Live UI Audit, Nov 19): a plain `<form method="post">` in main or a side panel redraws in place by itself (MAP.md › Live table connection); write a form as usual and it keeps scroll, tabs and what was open. Mark it `data-reload` only when its change shows outside main (the menu bar, body's settings).
- One shared stylesheet with the tokens above as CSS variables; pages don't define their own colors or sizes, and `tests/test_design_system.py` checks it.
- Every interactive element has a visible focus style and an accessible label.
- A time of day is written with the `clock` filter (`{{ when | clock }}`, `clock(date=True)` with the day): the server keeps UTC and doesn't know the reader's time zone, so the page shows it in the reader's own (app.js "clocks"). Never `strftime` a clock time; how long ago something was is `ago`.
- Mockups (archived in `docs/mockups/archive/`) and the living component page use made-up content only.

## Section headings (2026-10-18)
Section headings are all-caps, letter-spaced labels in amber with a fading rule (`h3.divider`). Only the action economy is color-coded, the same everywhere: ◆ red **Action**, ▲ amber **Bonus action**, ↺ blue **Reaction**, ● violet **Other** (classes `k-action`, `k-bonus`, `k-reaction`, `k-other`). Don't add more colors for other kinds of sections; the point is that color means "what it costs on your turn".

## Lists and filters (Brett 2026-10-21)
Every list of game things (spells, Wild Shape forms, inventory, features) follows one pattern. The Campaign page is for reading, not deciding, so it uses the Reader (Components) with the same engine and no filter column. Desktop first; phones get the same list with a **Filters (N)** sheet and no phone-only features.

- **Finding things in a list: four controls, one job each** (Brett, Nov 16: so people never wonder which does what). The same on every list, in this order from the top:
  1. **Search** (`search_box()`): finds by name or words, through everything; it pauses the filters and says so.
  2. **View** (the switch, `view_switch()`: All · Shortlist · Prepared · Last session): which set of rows you're looking at. Two to four, always a switch, never chips or a dropdown.
  3. **Filters** (chips in groups, `chip_group()`): narrow the set by what things are; OR within a group, AND across groups, a live count on each, **Clear** by their heading. Two layouts of one component: **beside the list** (`filter_column()`, a left column, where there's room: the Spell Book, Sheet's lists, the Item browser, Change spells, Party) and **above the list** (`filters_above()`; Brett chose A, Nov 16, `docs/mockups/filters-above/` round 2: in a narrow column, the Reader's World and Notes you sent, Battle's See all and + Add): a control drawn like Sort's dropdown, "Filters 3 ▾", that opens the chip groups beneath it; closed, what's on shows beside it as blue pills, each with its × (two at most, then "+1", which opens it), and Clear at the end. Regular weight, never a heading; two lines at most and never wider than its column (tests/browser/test_layout.py checks every place, at a laptop's and a phone's width, with one and every filter on); open or closed is remembered per list. A phone folds both.
  4. **Sort** (a dropdown labelled **Sort**, `.ls-select` with `data-ls-sort`): the order, beside the list's count, under the filters. Always a dropdown, never a switch, however few the choices (Notes' Newest first · Oldest first).
- **Main view decides across many items; the side panel shows one item.** Clicking a row opens the side panel from the right edge; inside a modal such as the Spell Book it slides out from the modal's own right edge, inside it. The Spell Book fills the window so rows aren't cramped. Pin, Shortlist, Remind me and the note are always available in the side panel; Sheet may also offer them inline on rows, and the Spell Book offers Pin on each row (an empty spell bar shows **+ Add**, which opens it).
- **Views:** **All first, in every switch**, then what's yours (Prepared, Known, Equipped, Yours), then **Shortlist**: All · Prepared · Shortlist on Sheet › Spells, All · Known on Sheet › Wild Shape, All · Equipped · Shortlist on Inventory, All · Shortlist on Features and Plan, All · Yours · Shortlist in Change spells, All · Last session on World (Brett, Nov 16, feedback 69). A list starts on All the first time, then on the view you left it on, on that device; the order never changes. The label is just **All** (never "All you can learn": a Beast you can't take yet is listed, locked, with why).
- **Filters:** a left column of chip groups, then a **More filters** fold. Chips are OR within a group, AND across groups; a second click turns a chip off; no "Any" chip. Each chip shows a live count; zero-count chips are greyed out but stay clickable (to be judged once live). **Clear** sits at the right of the **Filters** heading (`filter_head()`, `clear_filters()`), and on a phone's folded Filters button, only once something is filtered; the blue chips say what's on, so there's no row above the list (Brett chose A, Nov 16, `docs/mockups/filter-clear/`; his feedback 67). Every filtered list uses these pieces, the Reader's narrow column (World, Notes you sent) and Battle's See all and + Add panels too: one component, no list with filters of its own.
- **Search overrides filters** and says so (the filters dim while searching).
- **Sort and lenses are separate from filters.** Spells sort by level, then name. A lens (the Spell Book's **Cast at**: Base, 1st, 2nd…) changes numbers, not which rows show; its column is **Damage / Healing**, with healing in green. Sort options are single words (Level, Name, Amount).
- **Remembered per page, per browser**, never shared across pages, and cleared after a day without use.
- **Rows are compact**, one line plus an optional note line. Wild Shape rows get a thumbnail.
- **Spell filters:** visible Level, Effect (damage, heal, control, support, utility), Casting time, Concentration. Under More: Ritual, Save type, On a successful save (half, nothing, damage still lands), Attack roll, Area or single target, Damage type, Range. Save spells always say what a successful save does.
- **The DM** sees only the All view: no Ready, Shortlist or pins, and no notes.
- **Build order:** 1) words and one note per thing, 2) Spell Book, 3) Sheet spells, 4) Plan, 5) Wild Shape; later Inventory, Features and Campaign. Each step ships on its own, and Brett reviews it before the next.
